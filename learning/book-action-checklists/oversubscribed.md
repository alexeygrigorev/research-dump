# Oversubscribed — Action Checklist

**Daniel Priestley · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

Actions reconstructed from summaries of this specific book and corroborating explanations by Priestley. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every exercise in the full text.

**Navigation:** this uses the second edition's eight principles, five campaign phases, and closing business/team sections. Headings are shortened paraphrases. Earlier-edition summaries have seven principles and emphasize delivery in phase 4; their material is mapped by topic, not copied as the second edition's numbering. [O] [G]

**Use:** change `- [ ]` to `- [x]` when finished. **Practice** is an action; **Reflection** is a decision or written answer; **Option** is an alternative, not an obligation to try everything. These labels and task divisions are editorial. First work through the principles; repeat the campaign phases for a particular offer.

## Part I — Principles

### Principle 1 — Demand, capacity, and profitability

- [ ] **Practice:** Compare qualified demand with delivery capacity; identify excess capacity, balance, or excess demand. [G]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Accept that protecting service quality can mean some potential buyers miss out. [C]

### Principle 2 — Focus on your own audience

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify people who value your particular strengths more than the wider market does. [C]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether those people can afford your offer. [D]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether you genuinely want to serve this group. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Build familiarity with that audience rather than trying to become known to everyone. [C]

### Principle 3 — Build the market before making the sale

- [ ] **Practice:** Choose which demand driver your offer can credibly emphasize. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Compete through an innovation that existing alternatives do not provide. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Compete through relationships and individualized attention. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Compete through convenience by removing customer friction. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Compete through lower prices supported by genuine operating efficiencies. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Educate or entertain prospective customers before pressing for a purchase. [C]

### Principle 4 — Create the conditions for buying

- [ ] **Practice:** Clarify the practical reason someone would buy. [I]
- [ ] **Practice:** Connect the offer with an outcome the customer personally wants. [I]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify a genuine reason to act now rather than inventing urgency. [I]
- [ ] **Practice:** Show relevant customer success instead of relying entirely on your own sales claims. [A]

### Principle 5 — Differentiate and establish your rules

- [ ] **Practice:** Explain the philosophy or larger change behind your business, beyond its feature list. [A]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Choose a meaningful difference rather than copying competitors by default. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** State the working standards customers must accept. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Decline poor-fit work instead of weakening those standards to win every sale. [E]
- [ ] **Practice:** Be honest about limitations rather than promising immediate availability. [E]

### Principle 6 — Design a connected ecosystem of value

- [ ] **Practice:** Map how your core offer, complementary products, education, community, and support reinforce one another. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Share useful ideas that demonstrate your expertise. [A]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make the paid implementation of those ideas clear. [A]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give prospects a small, low-risk first step. [C]
- [ ] **Option:** Divide a substantial implementation into manageable stages. [C]
- [ ] **Practice:** Improve the surrounding ecosystem without discarding a core experience customers already value. [E]

### Principle 7 — Match the customer's situation and language

- [ ] **Option:** Use a survey, quiz, or scorecard to learn about prospective customers. [C]
- [ ] **Practice:** Learn their current situation and the result they want. [E]
- [ ] **Practice:** Group responses by meaningful differences in needs or readiness. [E]
- [ ] **Practice:** Tailor the explanation of your offer to those differences. [E]
- [ ] **Practice:** Connect your message with customers' existing priorities and values. [E]

### Principle 8 — Make the experience worth recommending

- [ ] **Practice:** Allocate resources to improving existing customers' experiences, not only attracting new ones. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify the people whose expertise and conduct should build trust in your business. [D]
- [ ] **Option:** Make their work visible through relevant writing, video, talks, or events. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Train the team to deliver the experience your positioning promises. [S]

## Part II — The campaign-driven method

### Phase 1 — Plan capacity, audience, and delivery

- [ ] **Practice:** Write the number of customers this campaign can genuinely delight: ________. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Define the qualifying characteristics of customers suited to this particular release. [I]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish a first purchase from the longer client relationship you hope to build. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose a campaign theme your intended audience will care about. [K]
- [ ] **Practice:** Build a timeline covering preparation, launch, delivery, and review. [K]
- [ ] **Practice:** Place your campaigns on a twelve-month schedule. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check the delivery resources needed before promoting the available places. [A]

### Phase 2 — Build anticipation and gather signals

- [ ] **Practice:** Announce what you intend to offer before asking people to buy. [C]
- [ ] **Practice:** Invite a low-commitment expression of interest. [I]
- [ ] **Option:** Create a waiting list for the upcoming offer. [I]
- [ ] **Option:** Use an introductory workshop to attract and educate interested people. [I]
- [ ] **Option:** Offer a report, sample, or other useful prospect resource. [A]
- [ ] **Practice:** Publish clear availability and participation terms. [I]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make the campaign information usable on mobile devices. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prepare useful material in formats your audience can readily consume. [A]
- [ ] **Option:** Audit your content against Priestley's 7–11–4 heuristic: seven hours, eleven interactions, four contexts. [I]
- [ ] **Practice:** Continue relationship-building after a person first signals interest. [C]

### Phase 3 — Measure demand and release deliberately

- [ ] **Practice:** Keep a shared scoreboard of interest signals. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish casual interest, informed engagement, and stronger commitments. [B]
- [ ] **Option:** Compare interest with the book's planning multiples: 5× strong signals, 10× engaged ideal prospects, or 100× weak interest relative to capacity. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Build more relevant interest when the release is not yet sufficiently supported. [K]
- [ ] **Practice:** Publish truthful demand and availability information. [I]
- [ ] **Practice:** Apply the selection criteria announced to prospective customers. [I]
- [ ] **Option:** Release capacity in stages instead of making everything available simultaneously. [A]
- [ ] **Option:** Offer genuinely distinct editions or packages to sustain interest. [A]
- [ ] **Option:** Use planned, genuine pricing deadlines rather than repeatedly resetting promotions. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Honor the stated capacity when demand rises. [I]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep appropriate marketing running after becoming busy instead of restarting from zero later. [I]

### Phase 4 — Follow through on sales and delivery

- [ ] **Practice:** Set a consistent rhythm for sales conversations and follow-ups. [J]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use a repeatable conversation structure rather than unplanned small talk. [J]
- [ ] **Practice:** Practice and improve selling as a professional skill. [J]
- [ ] **Practice:** Clarify expectations and disqualify unsuitable work before agreeing a sale. [J]
- [ ] **Practice:** Collect the details needed to complete the customer's purchase. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explicitly ask for the agreed payment. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** Audit customer touchpoints on a 1–10 scale. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose specific experience improvements to make each month. [B]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Notice where an interaction lifts, drains, or leaves customer enthusiasm unchanged. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Deliver the promises already made before relying on promotional extras. [W]
- [ ] **Option:** Add a relevant surprise such as a thank-you note, small gift, or useful sample. [W]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep customer interaction records accessible. [A]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use systems to manage delivery and follow-up reminders. [A]

### Phase 5 — Celebrate, learn, and improve

- [ ] **Practice:** Collect customer outcomes and genuine campaign stories. [A]
- [ ] **Practice:** Measure the campaign's actual results. [K]
- [ ] **Practice:** Debrief successes, mistakes, and improvements with the team. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use feedback to choose the next product or service improvement. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Share relevant successes with the audience for the next campaign. [W]
- [ ] **Practice:** Recognize the people who contributed. [A]
- [ ] **Practice:** Schedule recovery before immediately launching another campaign. [A]

## Interpretation and coverage

The checklist is a listening companion, not a promise that excess interest guarantees profit. Capacity is the volume you can serve well, not a sales aspiration. An expression of interest is not an order; an order is not necessarily collected revenue.

The numerical demand multiples and 7–11–4 framework above are the author's planning heuristics—not universal conversion rates, research sample requirements, or empirically established thresholds for trust. The demand categories represent different signal strengths, not quantities to add together as confirmed buyers. Use actual campaign outcomes to judge their relevance. [B] [I]

The closing reflection does not claim to decode the book's cryptic final passage. No definitive answer was established by the consulted sources. The team section assigns responsibilities; it is not a claim that every reader must immediately employ four people. Sales follow-through and delivery remain together under phase 4, following the second-edition contents. [G] [O]

The author interviews corroborate topics in the book; they are not presented as original-book excerpts. In particular, the later sales discussion supplies explanations of rhythm, structure, and professional practice already named in the second-edition contents. Its additional frameworks are not imported as extra book chapters. [J] [O]

This does not reconstruct every case study, sales script, or exercise in the second edition. Publisher-created examples, quizzes, unrelated newsletter content, and advice attributed to other books are excluded. Claims about guaranteed profitability, universally superior advertising methods, or fixed psychological reactions are not treated as established facts.

**Implementation safeguards—not additional claimed book assignments:** communicate genuine capacity limits, deadlines, and demand; never fabricate queues, scarcity, testimonials, or countdowns. Obtain permission to share customer stories, minimize collected personal data, respect contact preferences, and make payment and delivery terms accurate. Useful customer surprises do not justify misleading delivery promises or unsustainable extra work.
