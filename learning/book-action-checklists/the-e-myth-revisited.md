# The E-Myth Revisited — Action Checklist

**Michael E. Gerber · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

Actions reconstructed from summaries of this specific book. The nineteen headings follow its main chapter sequence; narrative chapters contain reflections rather than invented formal exercises. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every instruction in the original. [EB] [EC]

**Use:** mark completed actions with `- [x]`. **Practice** turns reported advice into a task; **Reflection** is a decision or written answer; **Option** is an alternative or case-study adaptation. Labels and task splitting are editorial. The franchise prototype is a design exercise—not an instruction to sell franchises or build a large chain.

## Part I — The E-Myth and American Small Business

## 1. The Entrepreneurial Myth

- [ ] **Reflection:** Separate knowing your craft from knowing how to operate its business. [ED]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify business responsibilities your technical expertise does not automatically cover. [ED]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Examine whether escaping a boss is your only plan for business ownership. [EW]

## 2. The Entrepreneur, the Manager, and the Technician

- [ ] **Practice:** Sort your work into future direction, organization, and technical delivery. [ED]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Identify which of those three roles dominates your decisions. [ED]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give neglected planning and organizing work an explicit place in your responsibilities. [ED]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Examine where the three roles pull the business in conflicting directions. [EW]

## 3. Infancy: The Technician's Phase

- [ ] **Practice:** List the activities that stop whenever you are absent. [ED]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Identify where customer demand exceeds what you can personally deliver. [ED]
- [ ] **Practice:** Recognize owner exhaustion as an operating-design problem, not only a motivation problem. [ED]

## 4. Adolescence: Getting Some Help

- [ ] **Practice:** Define an employee's expected result before handing over responsibility. [ES]
- [ ] **Practice:** Provide direction and follow-up rather than delegating through abandonment. [ES]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether hiring has reduced dependence on you or merely relocated confusion. [ED]

## 5. Beyond the Comfort Zone

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify capabilities and systems the next stage requires you to develop. [ED]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Examine whether shrinking back to doing everything yourself resolves the underlying problem. [ED]
- [ ] **Practice:** Describe how work should function when you cannot personally inspect every detail. [EW]

## 6. Maturity and the Entrepreneurial Perspective

- [ ] **Reflection:** Describe the completed business you intend to create, not only today's tasks. [EF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Start from the customer's desired experience when designing how it works. [ET]
- [ ] **Practice:** Align present operating choices with that intended business. [ED]
- [ ] **Reflection:** View the organization as an integrated system rather than a collection of jobs. [EW]

## Part II — The Turn-Key Revolution: A New View of Business

## 7. The Turn-Key Revolution

- [ ] **Reflection:** Treat the business's operating model as something you must design deliberately. [ER]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify which parts of your results could be reproduced without your personal improvisation. [ER]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish a successful individual performance from a repeatable business result. [ER]

## 8. The Franchise Prototype

- [ ] **Practice:** Use the current business as a testing ground for a repeatable model. [ER]
- [ ] **Practice:** Test a way of working before declaring it the standard. [ER]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check that the model benefits customers, staff, and other essential participants. [ER]
- [ ] **Practice:** Design tasks so appropriately trained people can perform them consistently. [ER]
- [ ] **Practice:** Document the tested method in an accessible operations manual. [ER]

## 9. Working On Your Business, Not In It

- [ ] **Practice:** Separate improving the business system from fulfilling today's orders. [EW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify recurring emergencies that need a system instead of repeated founder rescue. [ER]
- [ ] **Practice:** Define the consistently good experience customers should receive. [ER]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether presentation and operating standards reinforce that experience. [ER]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Ask whether another capable operator could reproduce the business from its documentation. [ER]

## Part III — Building a Small Business That Works!

## 10. The Business Development Process

### Innovation → Quantification → Orchestration

- [ ] **Practice:** Select a specific customer interaction or process to improve. [EC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Try a concrete alternative instead of relying only on discussion. [EC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record a baseline and measure the result of the change. [EC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Retain and document an improvement supported by the evidence. [EC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make the proven method repeatable across ordinary operations. [EC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Revisit standards when further testing reveals a better method. [EC]
- [ ] **Option:** Test a different greeting or presentation without assuming the book's anecdotal result will repeat. [EC]

## Interpretation and coverage

The main chapter sequence is retained, including the narrative final letter. Labels and actionable wording are adaptations. The seven-part development program is not seven separate companies or departments you must immediately hire for. A position agreement here describes responsibilities and results; it is not a substitute for a locally appropriate employment contract.

The franchise thought experiment does not impose a growth target. Historical survival percentages, fixed personality percentages, and claims about universal customer reactions to colors or gestures are not treated as current facts or guaranteed outcomes. Standards should make reliable work teachable; they do not remove the need for competence, judgment, or appropriate professional qualifications.

Bookey truncates some narrative sections. Other summaries provide overlapping coverage, not proof that every original exercise has been recovered. Reviewer commentary, unrelated-book material, quizzes, and publisher-created exercises are excluded. No original-book page numbers have been invented.
