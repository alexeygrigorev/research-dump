# Zero to Sold — Action Checklist

**Arvid Kahl · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

A practical reconstruction from summaries of this book, checked against its official contents, an authorized sample, and matching articles by the author. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every instruction in the original.

**Use:** mark completed actions with `- [x]`. **Practice** adapts reported advice into a task; **Reflection** is a decision or written answer; **Option** is an alternative, not a requirement. The labels and task splitting are editorial. Follow the stage relevant to your business; software, subscription, hiring, financing, and sale tasks are conditional.

**Chapter navigation:** the book has many short, unnumbered chapters. The headings below use shortened chapter names and sometimes group adjacent chapters. Printed-page start references follow the author's contents PDF—not the pagination of third-party summaries. Introductory and narrative passages are not padded with invented exercises. [ZT]

## On Bootstrapping

### What Is Bootstrapping? / The Four Stages — pp. 24, 27
- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide whether customer-funded independence fits your objectives better than investor-led expansion. [ZO]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Identify your current stage: Preparation, Survival, Stability, or Growth. [ZO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Focus your next work on that stage's main constraint rather than copying a later-stage business. [ZO]

## Part I — Preparation

### From Idea to Product / You Probably Have It Backwards — pp. 35, 37
- [ ] **Practice:** Write the sequence audience → critical problem → suitable solution → product before committing to an idea. [ZF]

### Bootstrapping an Audience / The Power of the Niche — pp. 41, 43
- [ ] **Practice:** List communities where your experience gives you access and credibility. [ZB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Define a narrowly identifiable group with shared goals, problems, and language. [ZN]
- [ ] **Practice:** State who belongs to the niche and who does not. [ZN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify the collective goal and the obstacles preventing members from reaching it. [ZN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check that members can pay for help, not merely express interest. [ZG]

### Deciding on a Market / Determining Its Size — pp. 52, 60
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify the buyer with purchasing authority separately from the eventual user. [ZM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Learn the industry's history, major participants, and attitude toward change. [ZM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask knowledgeable insiders which market-size sources they trust. [ZM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Triangulate a plausible market-size range instead of treating one estimate as certainty. [ZM]
- [ ] **Option:** For business markets, inspect industry reports, trade publications, conferences, and relevant podcasts. [ZM]
- [ ] **Option:** For freelancer or consumer markets, inspect communities, specialist audiences, and public statistics. [ZM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether the reachable market could support your business at plausible prices. [ZF]

### Identifying a Critical Problem / Finding the Most Critical One — pp. 69, 80
- [ ] **Practice:** List recurring problems that people cannot easily postpone or ignore. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify the time, money, or opportunity each problem costs. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record the workarounds or existing products customers already use. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prioritize the problem with meaningful consequences over a merely interesting inconvenience. [ZF]

### Problem Validation: Talking to the Right People — p. 93
- [ ] **Practice:** Recruit experienced practitioners who face the problem and influence spending. [ZP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask about actual recent work and difficulties before mentioning your proposed solution. [ZP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use a conversation that allows follow-up questions rather than relying only on a questionnaire. [ZP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Redirect compliments about your idea toward evidence of the person's real problem. [ZP]
- [ ] **Practice:** When someone requests a feature, investigate the underlying need and assumptions. [ZP]
- [ ] **Practice:** When complaints multiply, ask which obstacle matters most. [ZP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask unsuitable interviewees to introduce a more relevant person. [ZP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review your notes afterward for patterns and leading questions you accidentally asked. [ZP]
- [ ] **Option:** Announce your research in the community before contacting individual members. [ZP]

### A Solution Isn't a Product… Yet / Solution Validation / Asking the Right Questions — pp. 103, 105, 112
- [ ] **Practice:** Describe the desired outcome before deciding what software or service to build. [ZF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare possible solutions with prospective customers, including how each fits their existing workflow. [ZF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask about their current state, desired state, and obstacles. [ZG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether your solution creates new work or problems elsewhere in their process. [ZF]

### Getting Your Hands Dirty / The Finished-Product Myth / Minimum Viable Product — pp. 117, 119, 131
- [ ] **Practice:** Define the smallest complete version that solves the core problem. [ZV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Include the essential path to access the product, receive value, and pay. [ZV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Remove features that do not contribute to that path. [ZV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Match the interface to your audience's technical comfort. [ZV]
- [ ] **Option:** Start with one relevant platform or integration instead of supporting every possibility. [ZV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Put the working version in front of early customers and seek meaningful payment. [ZV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use their real usage and feedback to decide the next improvement. [ZV]

### How to Release: Often, Early, and Safely — p. 140
- [ ] **Practice:** Release small, focused changes instead of accumulating a large feature bundle. [ZR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether each change actually improves the customer's workflow. [ZR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Test with realistic data volumes, not only a tiny development account. [ZR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prepare a reliable rollback to the last working release. [ZR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep versioned release artifacts and account for database changes when planning rollback. [ZR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Automate repeatable build, test, and deployment steps. [ZR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Schedule nonurgent releases outside peak usage, with time available to handle problems. [ZR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Update customer documentation and operating instructions when behavior changes. [ZR]
- [ ] **Option:** Use staged rollouts or feature switches when they reduce a demonstrated release risk. [ZR]

### Successful Products / Not in House / Making Tech Choices — pp. 152, 158, 166
- [ ] **Practice:** Prefer proven, familiar technology over novelty that adds avoidable risk. [ZG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Buy standard infrastructure; customize what creates distinctive value. [ZG]

### A Well-Oiled Machine / Forget Goals, Create Systems — pp. 175, 177
- [ ] **Practice:** Turn recurring work into repeatable processes rather than relying on motivation or memory. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether those processes remain reliable when workload rises or something fails. [ZS]

### Your Initial Pricing — p. 184
- [ ] **Practice:** Estimate customer value and choose a credible initial price, not an automatic bargain price. [ZF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Test willingness to pay with customers and expect the price to evolve. [ZF]

### Do You Need a Co-Founder? — p. 190
- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare the benefits of complementary skills with shared control and shared proceeds. [ZC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Discuss goals, roles, working methods, and enthusiasm for the problem with a potential partner. [ZC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Test how you work together rather than relying only on friendship. [ZC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Agree how you will communicate and resolve overlapping responsibilities. [ZC]

## Part II — Survival

### Mental Health / Real and Imaginary Responsibilities — pp. 205, 207
- [ ] **Practice:** Protect recovery time and maintain people you can speak to about founder stress. [ZS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Separate genuine business obligations from perfectionism and imagined expectations. [ZG]

### Building Under Constraints / Feature Prioritization / Value, Not Applause — pp. 217, 221, 222, 229
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep a visible backlog of requested features, fixes, and improvements. [ZPR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Select a prioritization method that compares customer value with implementation effort. [ZPR]
- [ ] **Option:** Use RICE: estimate reach, impact, confidence, and effort to compare candidates. [ZPR]
- [ ] **Option:** Use DIE: compare demand, impact, and effort. [ZPR]
- [ ] **Option:** Use Kano: distinguish necessities, performance improvements, and genuine delights. [ZPR]
- [ ] **Option:** Use story mapping: organize customer workflow steps into useful release slices. [ZPR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Time-box prioritization and revisit it as evidence changes; do not let scoring replace building. [ZPR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Spend limited resources on essential customer value rather than attractive extras. [ZB]

### The Evolution of the How / Abstractions / Flexible Architecture — pp. 237, 239, 245
- [ ] **Practice:** Isolate dependencies to make services replaceable. [ZG]

### Customer Service with Minimum Effort — p. 253
- [ ] **Practice:** Answer recurring questions once in a reusable knowledge base. [ZH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Offer clear self-service help before requiring a conversation. [ZH]
- [ ] **Option:** Use short demonstrations when a workflow is easier to show than describe. [ZH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish self-service, asynchronous support, and situations requiring a live conversation. [ZH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Preserve customer context so each support interaction does not start from zero. [ZH]

### Re-Evaluating Your Audience — p. 261
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare unhappy customers with satisfied ones to detect an audience mismatch. [ZG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Reconsider your audience when dissatisfaction is widespread. [ZG]

### Churn, Retention, and Revenue — p. 267
- [ ] **Practice:** Track cancellations and retention alongside new sales. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask departing customers why they left. [ZG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Improve onboarding and remind customers of the value they receive. [ZS]

### Determining Product Value / Plans That No Longer Work — pp. 277, 284
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare each plan's revenue with its support burden and customer fit. [ZE]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide whether to retire an unsuitable plan, migrate customers, or grandfather existing subscriptions. [ZE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Tell affected customers about changes in advance, including people still on trials. [ZE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain the transition and respond individually to genuine concerns. [ZE]
- [ ] **Option:** Use a targeted concession where appropriate rather than discounting indefinitely for everyone. [ZE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Evaluate actual purchasing behavior, not only the loudest complaints. [ZE]

### Yearly Plans / Pricing Models That Can Break Your Business — pp. 287, 290
- [ ] **Practice:** Offer an annual subscription alongside monthly billing where the model fits. [ZY]
- [ ] **Option:** Test an annual discount that makes financial sense for the business. [ZY]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make billing and refund terms visible before purchase. [ZY]
- [ ] **Practice:** Plan how annual prepayments could fund improvements beyond the next month. [ZY]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check long-term support costs before offering free tiers or lifetime deals. [ZG]

### Referral Systems — p. 297
- [ ] **Practice:** Design a referral offer that benefits the business, the referrer, and the referred customer. [ZG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check that the referral mechanism suits your community's habits. [ZG]

### Recession Resilience — p. 310
- [ ] **Practice:** Review essential expenses and protect a cash buffer against weaker revenue. [ZB]

### Shoestring Marketing / Whales / Being Small / Transparency — pp. 325, 331, 334, 340
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose channels where your specific audience already spends attention. [ZB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Publish useful niche content instead of relying only on promotional messages. [ZB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Participate helpfully in relevant communities and make your offer easy to understand. [ZL]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare results across marketing activities before increasing spending. [ZB]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether a large prospect's demands would overwhelm you. [ZG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use personal responsiveness and close customer contact as advantages of being small. [ZB]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Reassess transparency as competitive exposure grows. [ZG]

## Part III — Stability

### Customer Service at Scale / Customer Exploration / Customer Retention — pp. 353, 357, 361
- [ ] **Practice:** Expand self-service material around recurring support questions. [ZH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Observe how customers actually use the product, not only what they request. [ZF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use feedback and usage patterns to improve the experience before pursuing more acquisition. [ZS]

### Continuous Validation — p. 369
- [ ] **Practice:** Recheck whether the original problem remains important as the market changes. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review whether current positioning, features, and pricing still fit customer needs. [ZS]

### Roadmaps and You — p. 376
- [ ] **Practice:** Treat roadmap votes as research leads, not automatic commitments to build. [ZG]

### Building a Sellable Business / Standard Operating Procedures — pp. 381, 386
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify essential tasks that currently depend on you alone. [ZF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Document recurring operations so another person can perform them. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Maintain instructions as the process changes, rather than treating documentation as finished. [ZF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Delegate or automate repeatable work that prevents you from operating sustainably. [ZB]

### An Extensible Product / The Power of Omission — pp. 393, 402
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify tools customers already use that your product should work alongside. [ZF]
- [ ] **Option:** Provide suitable imports, exports, or an API to fit existing workflows. [ZF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review low-value features for removal. [ZG]

### Growing Beyond the Founder(s) — p. 413
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify which responsibilities exceed your capacity before deciding whom to hire. [ZB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Document expectations and train new contributors in your operating processes. [ZB]

### A Tribe / Positioning / A Unified Voice — pp. 421, 426, 431
- [ ] **Practice:** Create ways for customers with shared goals to connect and help one another. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Clarify whom the product serves and why it is different. [ZS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep the business's voice consistent across contributors and customer touchpoints. [ZS]

## Part IV — Growth

### Should You Sell? / Different Kinds of Exits — pp. 437, 441, 443
- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare selling with keeping the business against your personal and financial objectives. [ZL]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish leaving ownership from stepping away from day-to-day operations. [ZL]

### Preparing for the Sale: Documentation — p. 448
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep organized business records in a secure, transferable location. [ZE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Separate business accounts, identities, and credentials from personal ones. [ZE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep an operations manual and a clear explanation of how documentation is organized. [ZL]
- [ ] **Practice:** Retain financial, customer, and process records that explain how the business operates. [ZS]

### Priming for Due Diligence — p. 453
- [ ] **Practice:** Prepare current financial statements and controlled access to evidence supporting business performance. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep operations running while buyer questions and negotiations consume attention. [ZA]

### Due Diligence on Your Potential Acquirer — p. 459
- [ ] **Practice:** Verify the buyer's identity, acquisition history, and funding capacity. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Research prior acquisitions independently and ask the buyer for references. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Speak with former owners, including relevant people missing from the supplied reference list. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask about unexpected obligations, transition workload, and treatment of employees. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Understand whether the buyer's motivation is financial or strategic. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Assess operating style and compatibility, not only the offer amount. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Investigate unexplained reluctance to provide references or disproportionate requests for sensitive information. [ZA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Have appropriate advisers review commitments before you sign. [ZA]

### Earn-Outs — p. 471
- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish guaranteed proceeds from payments dependent on future targets and buyer-controlled decisions. [ZG]

### Life After the Sale / Keeping Your Company — pp. 475, 479
- [ ] **Reflection:** Plan for lost routines and identity after exiting, not only the financial result. [ZG]
- [ ] **Option:** Keep ownership while transferring operational responsibility instead of selling outright. [ZL]

### Investment Options / Taking Venture Capital — pp. 485, 489
- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare remaining self-funded with outside capital against your desired control, obligations, and growth path. [ZL]

## Coverage and interpretation notes

This is a stage-by-stage companion, not a command to build a software subscription business, hire people, raise capital, or sell. Adjacent chapters are grouped where summaries discuss them together. A checkbox under a chapter means the action matches that topic in the cited material; it does not assert that the exact task appears verbatim on the printed page.

The official guide is a precursor/companion, not the complete published book. Author articles are used only for topics matching the published contents. Bookey's thematic seven-part summary is not the book's actual chapter structure. Reviewer commentary, unrelated-book advice, quizzes, and invented example exercises are excluded.

The authorized sample's introductory description places its subscription-plan extract differently; this checklist follows the official contents, which puts that chapter in Survival. The recession entry reflects the summaries' cash-resilience guidance, not a separate line-by-line reconstruction of that chapter.

Operational safeguards, not extra claimed book exercises: obtain consent before recording interviews; protect customer data during research and diligence; check current local requirements with qualified advisers before implementing billing, contracts, or a sale. The author's historical refund examples are not treated as universal legal rules. Technical implementation must fit the actual product; a rollback plan alone does not guarantee data recovery.
