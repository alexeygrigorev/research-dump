# Million Dollar Weekend — Action Checklist

**Noah Kagan with Tahl Raz · English listening companion · Expanded summary-based edition**

This checklist reconstructs actions from summaries of this specific book and cross-checks named methods against the author’s explanations. It is not an official worksheet or a line-by-line inventory of the original text. Wording and task breakdown are adapted; no completeness claim is made beyond the material accessible in the cited sources.

**How to use:** follow the chapters while listening and mark completed items with `- [x]`. **Challenge** identifies a named exercise or a component of one; **Practice** turns reported advice into an action; **Option** identifies an alternative, optional tactic, or case-study adaptation. Do not treat every option as mandatory. Letters beside tasks link to sources; the source guide gives section locators. Blank amounts and dates are yours to choose, not numbers prescribed by the book.


## 1. Just Fu\*\*ing Start

### Start before you feel ready

- [ ] **Practice:** Choose a delayed business action and do its smallest useful version now. [L]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Email close friends now: what business would suit you? [R]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Write your Freedom Number: a meaningful initial monthly income target. Amount: ________. [O]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Ask someone you know for $1 toward your future business, offering progress updates in return. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Send the progress updates you promised in the Dollar Challenge. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review a failed attempt for lessons, then run another small experiment. [F]

## 2. The Unlimited Upside of Asking

### Practice asking and handling rejection

- [ ] **Practice:** Make a specific request you have avoided because you fear rejection. [L]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Set a rejection target and track your attempts: ________ refusals over ________. [D]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Coffee Challenge: politely request 10% off before paying, without inventing a justification. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** After asking, stop talking and let the other person answer. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Accept the coffee-shop response graciously; completing the ask, not receiving a discount, is the exercise. [D]
- [ ] **Practice:** Clarify whether a prospect means never or simply not now. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Follow up on an unresolved conversation rather than abandoning it after one attempt. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain how your offer helps the customer, instead of treating selling as imposing. [B]
- [ ] **Option:** Leave an honest book review, as the author requests. [B]

## 3. Finding Million-Dollar Ideas

### Start with reachable customers

- [ ] **Practice:** Map your Zone of Influence: friends, colleagues, communities, and groups you already belong to. [O]
- [ ] **Practice:** For each idea, specify who buys, which problem you solve, and where those buyers are. [O]
- [ ] **Practice:** Talk to those people about their problems before deciding what product to build. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** List frustrations you experience and problems you observe other people struggling with. [T]

### Explore the book’s four idea sources

- [ ] **Practice:** Review irritating tasks that remain unfinished on your to-do list. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Inspect bestselling products and identify a useful accessory you could offer. [G]
- [ ] **Option:** Consider a service teaching buyers how to use a popular product. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Browse marketplace demand, including completed eBay sales, rather than only sellers’ asking prices. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check Google autocomplete for problems people are searching to solve. [R]
- [ ] **Option:** Explore question searches with AnswerThePublic and requests in r/SomebodyMakeThis. [G]

### Build and narrow the idea list

- [ ] **Challenge:** Run an idea sprint: generate at least ten candidates in ten minutes. [O]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare candidates for personal enthusiasm, business potential, and ease of implementation. [T]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Shortlist three ideas for the One-Minute Business Model. [O]

## 4. The One-Minute Business Model

### Check the opportunity and the arithmetic

- [ ] **Practice:** Check market interest with Google Trends: declining, stable, or rising. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Estimate market size; the book illustrates this with Facebook Ads audience estimates. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose an initial selling price using comparable offers as a reference. [V]
- [ ] **Practice:** Estimate delivery cost per sale, then calculate price minus cost. [V]
- [ ] **Practice:** Divide your target profit by profit per sale to estimate required sales. [V]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Repeat the calculation for $1 million, keeping revenue and profit distinct. [V]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether enough plausible buyers exist and whether you can reach them. [V]

### Try the six revenue dials when the model looks weak

- [ ] **Option:** Increase order size through a larger package or bundle. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Increase purchase frequency through repeat purchases or subscriptions. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Explore a different price point. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Change customer type, such as individuals versus businesses. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Expand or alter the product line. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Add a complementary paid service. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Recalculate after changes; choose one workable idea for the weekend test. [T]

## 5. The 48-Hour Money Challenge

### Prepare a real purchase test

- [ ] **Challenge:** Set the 48-hour test window: start ________; finish ________. [O]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Create your Dream Ten: ten reachable people who plausibly need the offer. [T]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prepare a simple way to accept payment before polishing branding or building the product. [V]
- [ ] **Practice:** For a presale, explain that you are launching and agree when buyers will receive it. [V]

### Use Listen → Options → Transition

- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what frustrates the customer about their current way of doing things. [T]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask how solving that problem would improve their life. [T]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what they think a solution should cost. [T]
- [ ] **Practice:** Discuss alternative solutions and learn which option interests them most. [T]
- [ ] **Practice:** Build the offer around a price, a concrete benefit, and a delivery time. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Transition from discussion to an explicit request to purchase. [T]

### Choose a validation route — alternatives, not three compulsory projects

- [ ] **Option:** Presell directly through conversations with people in your network. [V]
- [ ] **Option:** Publish an offer on a marketplace where relevant buyers already shop. [V]
- [ ] **Option:** Create a simple landing page with a purchase button and bring prospective buyers to it. [V]

### Learn from refusals and close the loop

- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what stopped the person from buying. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what change would make the offer an easy decision. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what they would pay for the revised offer. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask for one person who might genuinely need it. [G]
- [ ] **Practice:** Follow up with interested people who have not decided; respect a clear refusal. [D]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Obtain payment from three customers within 48 hours; compliments and promises do not count. [O]
- [ ] **Practice:** Fulfill what you sold rather than stopping at the successful presale. [V]
- [ ] **Practice:** When the test falls short, use feedback to revise the offer or try another idea. [T]

## 6. Social Media Is for Growth . . .

### Define your unique angle

- [ ] **Challenge:** Describe your distinctive experience and why people should listen. [B]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Describe your interests and how you will help others. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify a common misconception you can explain differently. [R]
- [ ] **Practice:** State your content promise as a useful outcome for a specific audience. [G]

### Choose a platform and develop your content

- [ ] **Practice:** Test suitable platforms for thirty days; focus on the strongest fit. [R]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose for audience fit and your preferred creative format. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Start your Core Circle: publish about a tightly defined audience and problem. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Expand into a Medium Circle of related topics and audiences. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Later explore a Large Circle: broader topics including your original audience. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Be a guide, not a guru: document your own experiments. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Share useful knowledge generously instead of making every interaction a sales pitch. [L]
- [ ] **Practice:** Treat early followers as people: respond and build conversations, not just a follower count. [L]

## 7. . . . Email Is for Profit

### Build the list and reach the first hundred subscribers

- [ ] **Practice:** Create a newsletter signup page with a clear reason to subscribe. [B]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Invite friends and family individually to get your first ten subscribers. [R]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Work toward fifty subscribers using email signatures and profile links. [R]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Use social content to reach one hundred subscribers. [R]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Create a useful free resource as a signup incentive. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Link relevant content to your signup page and free resource. [B]

### Set up the three-part autoresponder

- [ ] **Challenge:** Welcome email: introduce yourself and explain what subscribers should expect. [S]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Connection email: invite subscribers to connect with you on social media. [S]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Content email: send your strongest useful material to new subscribers. [S]

### Write, sell, and keep practicing

- [ ] **Practice:** Write an email with personality, storytelling, or natural humor. [B]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make a clear paid offer to your email audience. [R]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Law of 100: choose a goal-relevant activity and commit to one hundred repetitions. [O]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record completed repetitions; persist beyond a weak start rather than constantly switching activities. [O]

## 8. The Growth Machine

### Answer the five marketing-plan questions

- [ ] **Challenge:** Choose one measurable annual growth goal and a deadline. [B]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Identify the exact customer and where you can find them. [B]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Identify one marketing activity worth concentrating more effort on. [B]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Decide how to delight your first one hundred customers. [B]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Brainstorm how to double the business in thirty days with no marketing money. [B]

### Run marketing experiments and compare outcomes

- [ ] **Practice:** Ask customers where they would expect to discover a product like yours. [R]
- [ ] **Practice:** List possible marketing channels and forecast the sales each could produce. [R]
- [ ] **Practice:** Run the selected experiments for thirty days and record actual sales beside forecasts. [R]
- [ ] **Practice:** Concentrate resources on demonstrated winners; stop channels that fail the test. [S]
- [ ] **Option:** Consider niche-blogger outreach and useful content, drawing on the Mint example rather than copying it blindly. [L]

### Grow through existing customers

- [ ] **Practice:** Personally ask early customers what needs improvement, then act. [S]
- [ ] **Challenge:** Separately brainstorm doubling the business without adding any new customers. [G]

## 9. 52 Chances This Year

### Design the year you actually want

- [ ] **Challenge:** Write a Dream Year covering work, health, personal life, and travel. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check that those ambitions reflect your preferences, not someone else’s definition of success. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** Turn the vision into specific, attainable goals. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** Break annual goals into weekly milestones. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** Translate weekly milestones into concrete daily actions. [F]
- [ ] **Option:** Keep goals visible, such as on your phone wallpaper or a mirror. [F]
- [ ] **Practice:** Color-code your calendar by goal category and reserve time for those priorities. [S]

### Build accountability and relationships

- [ ] **Practice:** Choose an accountability partner and arrange weekly check-ins. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** At each check-in, review progress and agree on next week’s goals. [S]
- [ ] **Practice:** Connect with promising peers before fame: the prefluencer approach. [R]
- [ ] **Practice:** Begin relationships with appreciation, not an immediate request. [R]
- [ ] **Option:** Try Andrew Chen’s networking experiment: meet five new people per day. [R]
- [ ] **Option:** Adapt Andrew Chen’s example: after a conversation, send three useful takeaways. [R]
- [ ] **Option:** Ask for introductions to a couple of relevant people. [R]
- [ ] **Option:** Mention your mutual connection and offer genuine value. [R]
- [ ] **Practice:** After a setback, return to a small experiment instead of treating it as your final attempt. [L]

## Source checks and limits

The chapter order follows the official book overview. The following distinctions matter when comparing summaries:

- **Coffee discount:** Bookey’s separate illustrative example on PDF p. 13 uses 20%; the author specifies 10%, which is used here. [B] [D]
- **Scope:** Bookey truncates chapters 3, 6, and 9. Its quizzes, commentary, and illustrative scenes are not treated as author exercises. [B]
- **Attribution:** The priority criteria and some networking details come from reader summaries; the linked source identifies that level of evidence. Review-site material about other books is excluded.
- **Alternatives:** The three validation routes and six revenue dials are choices, not requirements to execute all of them. Case-study actions are labeled Option. Law of 100 is placed in chapter 7, following the official outline. [O]

Operational clarifications: request consent rather than silently adding newsletter contacts; respect refusals; describe presales truthfully. The arithmetic in chapter 4 is only a rough opportunity screen, not a forecast that everyone in a market will buy or a complete accounting model. These clarifications are not additional claimed book exercises.
