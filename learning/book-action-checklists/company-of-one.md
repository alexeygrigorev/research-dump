# Company of One — Action Checklist

**Paul Jarvis · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

A practical reconstruction from multiple summaries of this specific book, with selected points corroborated by Jarvis's interview about it. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every instruction in the original.

**Use:** mark completed actions with `- [x]`. **Practice** adapts reported advice into a task; **Reflection** is a decision or written answer; **Option** is an alternative. These labels, task breakdowns, and blank answer spaces are editorial—not named exercises invented by the author.

**Navigation:** the thirteen numbered headings follow the main book chapters. Prologue and afterword are not counted as extra numbered chapters. Some summaries regroup ideas; placement here is a topic-based mapping rather than a claim that each paraphrase occurs at an exact original-book location. [CB] [CD]

## 1. Defining a Company of One

- [ ] **Reflection:** Describe the life your work should support. [CD]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Identify where growth would undermine your autonomy or resilience. [CD]
- [ ] **Practice:** Assess your core skill honestly and strengthen it before depending on complete independence. [CF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose one recurring process to simplify instead of adding resources. [CU]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Write how you would adapt to a realistic setback without abandoning your purpose. [CS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish useful business growth from growth pursued mainly for status. [CP]

## 2. Staying Small as an End Goal

- [ ] **Reflection:** Define enough: desired income, customer load, and working hours. Your answer: ________. [CU]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Set both a minimum sustainable goal and an upper boundary beyond which more becomes undesirable. [CR]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Choose success measures beyond size: relationship quality, well-being, and resilience. [CR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Test whether the next expansion could be replaced by better use of existing resources. [CR]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Identify what you envy in another business—and whether you value it. [CD]
- [ ] **Practice:** Decline an opportunity whose added management work conflicts with the business you want. [CP]

## 3. What's Required to Lead

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify gaps in the general business skills you need beyond your specialist craft. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Practice explaining your direction clearly to customers and collaborators. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Listen for what collaborators need to do good work instead of controlling every step. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give capable specialists a clear outcome and room to choose their methods. [CP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Set communication expectations and availability boundaries before work begins. [CP]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare your offer with customer alternatives so you can explain its distinctive value. [CD]

## 4. Growing a Company That Doesn't Grow

- [ ] **Practice:** Improve the experience of existing customers before assuming you need more customers. [CD]
- [ ] **Practice:** Evaluate profitability separately from revenue growth. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review whether pricing, retention, or efficiency could improve results without a larger team. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose an improvement you can deliver with resources already available. [CR]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Assess an opportunity's continuing workload, not only its initial financial upside. [CP]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether increasing sales would force you into a job you no longer enjoy. [CD]

## 5. Determining the Right Mind-Set

- [ ] **Reflection:** State the purpose guiding your work beyond a desire for revenue. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use that purpose to make a real product, client, or operating decision. [CD]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Identify whether your interest is supported by useful skills and customer demand. [CF]
- [ ] **Option:** Build relevant skills and initial customers part-time before leaving existing employment. [CR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Remove busywork that does not serve the business's purpose or your customers. [CU]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Choose a business direction grounded in your strengths rather than chasing every appealing possibility. [CU]

## 6. Personality Matters

- [ ] **Reflection:** Describe the qualities you genuinely want customers to associate with your business. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Express those qualities consistently through your language and behavior. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Write positioning that appeals to a specific audience rather than trying to please everyone. [CU]
- [ ] **Practice:** Let relevant personal experience and perspective show in your communication. [CD]
- [ ] **Option:** State a genuine differentiating belief, accepting that it will not attract every customer. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check that your actions support your public personality instead of relying on a manufactured image. [CB]

## 7. The One Customer

- [ ] **Practice:** Speak directly with a customer about the outcome they are trying to achieve. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give that customer personal attention rather than treating them as an acquisition statistic. [CD]
- [ ] **Practice:** Improve something that helps an existing customer succeed, not merely buy again. [CR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask for feedback and act on a concrete point of friction. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Look for ways to exceed relevant expectations without promising unsustainable service. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep communicating after purchase instead of disappearing until the next sale. [CD]

## 8. Scalable Systems

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify work that can grow in reach without growing your working hours proportionally. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review systems for creation, customer connections, and collaboration. [CR]
- [ ] **Option:** Turn repeatedly delivered knowledge into a reusable product or educational resource. [CS]
- [ ] **Option:** Use automation for repetitive administration while preserving useful personal contact. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Simplify a process before assuming outsourcing or hiring will fix it. [CU]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether a proposed system actually reduces complexity and ongoing work. [CU]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use expert help where needed without treating smallness as an obligation to do everything yourself. [CP]

## 9. Teach Everything You Know

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify knowledge that helps your intended customers solve a relevant problem. [CD]
- [ ] **Practice:** Publish a useful explanation of how your approach or product creates value. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Teach customers how to use what they buy successfully. [CB]
- [ ] **Option:** Choose an educational format you can sustain: writing, demonstrations, workshops, or lessons. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Answer recurring audience questions instead of communicating only promotional claims. [CD]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use replies and conversations to discover what people need explained next. [CP]

## 10. Properly Utilizing Trust and Scale

- [ ] **Practice:** Check that your offer reliably solves the problem you promise to address. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make expectations clear instead of depending on exaggerated marketing. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Build on trust with existing customers before seeking anonymous scale. [CR]
- [ ] **Option:** Create a referral or loyalty benefit that suits your audience. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make it easy for satisfied customers to explain your value to other suitable people. [CD]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Assess whether increasing reach would weaken the trust on which the business depends. [CR]

## 11. Launching and Iterating in Tiny Steps

- [ ] **Reflection:** Define minimum viable profit: the level needed to sustain the business and your income. [CR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Reduce the initial offer to one audience and one important problem. [CU]
- [ ] **Practice:** Launch a small usable version before investing in a large operation. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Seek paying customers early enough to test demand, not only enthusiasm. [CR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use feedback from actual delivery to improve the next version. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep early expenses low so profitability does not require unnecessary scale. [CU]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide whether evidence supports improving the offer, changing direction, or stopping. [CD]

## 12. The Hidden Value of Relationships

- [ ] **Practice:** Listen for people's goals and shared interests before steering a conversation toward a sale. [CB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Offer useful help without requiring an immediate return. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Stay in contact with customers and peers outside launch or sales periods. [CP]
- [ ] **Option:** Collaborate with a complementary small business on a useful shared project. [CS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give others credit and recommend good work that serves your audience. [CS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Evaluate relationships by mutual value and trust, not simply by audience size. [CP]

## 13. Starting a Company of One—My Story

- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide whether you want the responsibilities of business ownership, not just the freedom it suggests. [CF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify the specialist skill or existing experience your initial offer can build on. [CF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use direct questions from your audience to shape a useful first offer. [CD]
- [ ] **Practice:** List essential business costs and the personal income the operation must support. [CR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Plan salary, savings, and the lifestyle limits your business needs to respect. [CR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Consult an appropriate accountant about bookkeeping, tax obligations, and business finances. [CP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Get qualified help with contracts and business structure instead of copying someone else's arrangements. [CP]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check that the resulting business still supports the life you described in chapter 1. [CU]

## Coverage and interpretation notes

A company of one can use collaborators. Grow when needed for sustainability; reassess expansion afterward. [CP] [CR]

Some of these chapters are reflective rather than collections of formal exercises. Their recommendations are therefore expressed as decisions, reviews, or actions—not falsely described as named challenges. No arbitrary publishing quotas, launch deadlines, income targets, or head-count limits have been added. Amounts and business sizes in the book's examples are not universal targets.

Bookey counts the prologue in its chapter numbering; this checklist numbers only the thirteen main chapters. Its quizzes, hypothetical examples, and critical-thinking sections are not used as author instructions. Reader-specific digressions in Dan Silvestre's article and Durmonski's separate editorial “Actionable Notes” are excluded. Shortform contributes only material visible in its public preview, not locked chapters or advice imported from other books.

Implementation safeguard, not an additional book claim: entity choice, liability, taxes, contracts, and subscription terms depend on the actual jurisdiction and circumstances. This checklist prompts professional review; it does not prescribe a legal structure or claim that one structure guarantees protection.
