# The SaaS Playbook — Action Checklist

**Rob Walling · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

Practical actions reconstructed from multiple summaries of this specific book, with the official sample used for navigation and selected author material used for corroboration. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every instruction in the original.

**Scope:** the book primarily addresses developing and growing a SaaS business with initial customer demand, not a complete idea-validation course. Do not treat later-stage acquisition, hiring, or funding actions as prerequisites for finding your first customers. [SP]

**Use:** mark completed actions with `- [x]`. **Practice** is an action; **Reflection** is a decision; **Option** is an alternative. Labels, task splitting, and shortened subsection headings are editorial. Alternatives such as acquisition channels, incentives, and funding routes are not requirements to try everything.

**Navigation:** the six main chapters and subsection start pages follow the official contents. Page references are printed-book pages, not PDF page numbers or audiobook timestamps. Nearby subsections are sometimes grouped. [ST]

## Opening framework — Goals and the bootstrapper's path

*Introductory material, pp. 19–39.*

- [ ] **Reflection:** Define the income, independence, and scale you actually want from the business. [SP]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide whether recurring software fits the kind of work and company you want to operate. [ST]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare self-funding, limited outside investment, and venture-style growth rather than treating funding as a binary choice. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify whether demand, retention, acquisition, or capacity currently limits growth. [SS]
- [ ] **Option:** Consider Stair Step: gain experience with simpler products before attempting standalone SaaS. [SS]

## 1. Market — p. 43

### Deepen product–market fit — p. 43

- [ ] **Practice:** Speak with prospects, current customers, people who declined, and former customers. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use those conversations to revise product, pricing, and marketing assumptions. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what a requested feature would let the customer accomplish. [SL]
- [ ] **Practice:** Estimate how much of the customer base would benefit from the request. [SL]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether the request fits your product's intended direction. [SL]
- [ ] **Practice:** Sort requests into clear necessities, distractions, and uncertain cases. [ST]
- [ ] **Option:** Use an integration instead of rebuilding a complementary capability. [ST]

### Compete deliberately; monitor without obsessing — pp. 51, 55

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify frustrations customers experience with established alternatives. [SJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose a meaningful difference in the product, purchasing experience, or pricing approach. [SJ]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Explain why your offer is more than a slightly altered copy of an incumbent. [SP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Track which competitors actually win your lost deals and why. [SJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use those findings to guide decisions instead of continually reacting to competitors' announcements. [SJ]

### Build defensibility — p. 57

- [ ] **Option:** Develop integrations that make the product more useful within customers' existing systems. [SJ]
- [ ] **Option:** Build recognition and trust with the audience you serve. [SJ]
- [ ] **Option:** Develop acquisition channels that competitors cannot instantly reproduce. [SJ]
- [ ] **Option:** Make the product valuable within important recurring workflows, increasing the benefit of staying. [SJ]

### Avoid premature expansion — p. 62

- [ ] **Reflection:** Evaluate translation's ongoing support and operating burden, not only the initial text conversion. [SJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Seek a credible partner commitment before investing in a speculative white-label version. [SJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Strengthen the current market before adding unrelated verticals. [SJ]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Recognize the added acquisition burden before turning a single-sided product into a marketplace. [SV]

## 2. Pricing — p. 67

### Design the plans — p. 67

- [ ] **Practice:** Segment customers by needs, usage, business size, and willingness to pay. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Create understandable tiers aligned with those segments. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose a value metric or feature boundaries that reflect what customers receive. [SF]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether low pricing leaves enough revenue to support acquisition and service. [SV]

### Let revenue expand with customer success — p. 70

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify a billable unit that can grow as customers gain more value. [SG]
- [ ] **Option:** Use seats, usage, or another appropriate growing quantity rather than a permanently flat fee. [SG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make higher-tier benefits and the upgrade path understandable. [SF]

### Assess free plans and trial qualification — pp. 73, 75

- [ ] **Reflection:** Assess free-plan support costs, reach, and the route to revenue. [SH]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether free usage generates distribution, not just unpaid work. [SH]
- [ ] **Option:** Test requiring a payment card at trial signup when stronger qualification could improve the business. [SV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Evaluate the resulting paying customers, not just the change in trial registrations. [SV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Include trial-support workload when comparing signup approaches. [SV]

### Revisit and increase prices — pp. 79, 80

- [ ] **Practice:** Review pricing as product value and customer knowledge evolve. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Notify affected customers before a price increase. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain the value supporting the increase. [SS]
- [ ] **Option:** Consider temporary grandfathering rather than permanently preserving every legacy price. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Observe actual purchases and cancellations after the change, not only objections voiced beforehand. [SV]

## 3. Marketing — p. 87

### Own acquisition and diagnose the funnel — pp. 87, 89

- [ ] **Practice:** Learn enough about acquisition to direct the work before outsourcing marketing strategy. [SL]
- [ ] **Practice:** Map discovery, signup, payment, and retention. [SU]
- [ ] **Practice:** Fix retention and conversion before buying more traffic. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Locate the drop-offs between funnel stages. [SU]

### Consider two sales paths — p. 93

- [ ] **Option:** Offer self-service to smaller buyers and assisted sales to larger accounts. [SS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether low-touch users help larger buyers discover you. [SS]

### Select acquisition experiments — pp. 95, 101

- [ ] **Option:** Test search visibility for problems your buyers already investigate. [SS]
- [ ] **Option:** Test paid acquisition where customer value can support the spending. [SF]
- [ ] **Option:** Test targeted outbound contact with a relevant offer. [SB]
- [ ] **Option:** Test integration partnerships that already reach your audience. [SB]
- [ ] **Option:** Publish useful content for the buyers you are trying to reach. [SB]
- [ ] **Option:** Consider audience-appropriate affiliates, events, partnerships, or free tools. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare channels by speed of feedback, total cost, and ability to scale. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Rank experiments by impact, confidence, and ease (ICE). [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Log each marketing change, its cost, and the outcome. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Increase investment in channels that work rather than repeatedly switching without evidence. [SF]

### Run useful sales demonstrations — p. 109

- [ ] **Practice:** Qualify need, budget, and timing before demonstrating. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask open questions about their current workflow and frustrations. [SB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify the buying decision-makers. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prepare a repeatable demo script. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Demonstrate the relevant solution, not every feature. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Help the buyer solve their problem without pressure. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Agree next steps and follow up. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Decline poor-fit sales instead of creating future churn. [SH]

## 4. Team — p. 117

### Assign ownership, not only isolated tasks — p. 117

- [ ] **Practice:** Map responsibility for product, design, engineering, marketing, sales, support, customer success, and operations. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify the highest-impact missing or overloaded role. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Hire for sustained responsibility rather than continually handing out disconnected tasks. [SL]
- [ ] **Option:** Combine compatible roles, not every discipline in one hire. [SB]

### Match management to the work — p. 127

- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish work needing detailed instructions from projects that need independent end-to-end delivery. [SM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Decide when a role requires ownership of an outcome and strategic judgment. [SM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Set the required autonomy level before assessing candidates. [SM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether all coordination and decisions still return to you despite adding people. [SM]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish administrative supervision from domain leadership. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Reassess management as coordination demands increase. [SS]

### Attract and retain good people — p. 131

- [ ] **Practice:** Describe the meaningful work, autonomy, and culture you can offer. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep the workplace promises made during hiring. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Address performance mismatches with timely feedback. [SH]

### Choose incentives deliberately — p. 135

- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide whether the incentive should reward current profit, long-term ownership value, retention, or a combination. [SE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare bonuses, profit sharing, options, and actual ownership before choosing a mechanism. [SE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make eligibility, calculation, and payout expectations understandable. [SE]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Assess whether options have a plausible route to value for the employee. [SE]
- [ ] **Option:** Use a defined profit-sharing pool instead of accumulating unrelated permanent individual promises. [SE]
- [ ] **Practice:** Get qualified legal and accounting review of the proposed arrangement. [SE]

### Decide whether a cofounder helps — p. 139

- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare the value of complementary skills with shared ownership and decision-making. [SV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Look for a partner who fills a real capability gap, not merely someone who resembles you. [SV]
- [ ] **Option:** Test collaboration through a small project before committing. [SH]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Keep the solo-founder route open when a partner would not improve the business. [SV]

## 5. 80/20 SaaS Metrics — p. 143

### Build a small, useful dashboard — pp. 143, 145

The framework favors higher contract value, expansion, and referrals; lower acquisition cost, sales effort, and churn. [SS]

- [ ] **Practice:** Track monthly recurring revenue and its month-to-month growth. [SV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Treat those as results to explain, not a complete diagnosis of the business. [SL]
- [ ] **Practice:** Track annual contract value and higher-value customer opportunities. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Track revenue gained when existing customers expand. [SG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Track referrals separately from paid acquisition. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare acquisition cost with acquired customer value. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Track sales effort per closed account. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Track churn alongside new business. [SV]

### Examine product-led distribution — p. 152

- [ ] **Reflection:** Identify whether ordinary product use exposes it to potential new customers. [SG]
- [ ] **Option:** Use relevant shared outputs, invitations, or public product surfaces to support discovery. [SG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish built-in distribution from a separate refer-a-friend reward. [SG]

### Find the causes of churn — p. 154

- [ ] **Practice:** Estimate the plateau: monthly new recurring revenue ÷ monthly revenue-churn fraction. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Shorten onboarding to the first meaningful customer result. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Segment churn by plan, acquisition source, and signup cohort. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Speak with people who leave to understand what failed to fit. [SF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Investigate poor-fit customer segments rather than assuming every cancellation requires another feature. [SV]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use retention findings when deciding which customers and channels to pursue. [SV]

### Work toward positive growth inside the customer base — p. 161

- [ ] **Practice:** Compare expansion revenue with revenue lost through cancellations and downgrades. [SG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether retained customers can grow the recurring base even without new signups. [SG]
- [ ] **Practice:** Address both avoidable losses and opportunities for customers to receive—and pay for—more value. [SG]

## 6. Mindset — p. 165

### Choose the work and use your time well — pp. 165, 168

- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether you enjoy serving this audience and solving its problems. [SP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prioritize consequential decisions over a merely full calendar. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Delegate predictable, repeatable work that does not need founder judgment. [SM]
- [ ] **Practice:** Reserve attention for uncertain, high-impact questions that could change the business's direction. [SM]

### Evaluate outside capital — p. 171

- [ ] **Practice:** Write what funding would pay for and what progress it should enable. [SC]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare funding benefits with dilution, obligations, and fundraising effort. [SC]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether money accelerates a working approach or merely postpones unresolved product demand. [SV]

### Respond to setbacks — p. 176

- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish a frustrating obstacle from a genuinely business-ending event. [SP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Seek an outside perspective before treating a setback as catastrophic. [SP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify alternative responses when the original path is blocked. [SP]

### Build a support network — p. 179

- [ ] **Option:** Join a founder group for feedback and accountability. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose mentors whose business and life choices resonate. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Discuss difficulties as well as successes with peers. [SH]

### Reduce burnout and step back — pp. 182, 186

- [ ] **Practice:** Transfer or redesign recurring responsibilities that drain you. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Allow meaningful recovery instead of uninterrupted work. [SS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Change exhausting work patterns; one break is not a lasting fix. [SB]
- [ ] **Option:** Take a quiet retreat away from routine work and information intake. [SH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review business direction, personal priorities, and neglected decisions. [SH]

## Coverage and interpretation notes

The official sample establishes the opening material and six-chapter structure; the rest is a reconstruction from accessible book summaries. The short headings and page locators are navigation aids, not claims that each paraphrased checkbox appears verbatim on that page. Narrative examples are not automatically converted into required exercises.

The author's older podcast transcripts below corroborate matching topics found in the book summaries. They are not presented as extracts from the published book. Only the relevant segments are used. The book-launch interview is used for Walling's discussion of the book, not unrelated host advice.

The plateau calculation is a simplified steady-state estimate: it assumes constant acquisition and a positive, constant revenue-loss rate, without expansion revenue. Enter a percentage as a fraction, such as 0.04 rather than 4. This is a calculation clarification, not a new book exercise or a forecast.

The checklist deliberately does not turn illustrative prices, staffing ratios, growth figures, or acquisition budgets into universal targets. Expansion revenue, built-in distribution, and dual funnels are conditional opportunities—not guaranteed growth mechanisms. “Net negative churn” concerns revenue retained and expanded within an existing customer base; it does not mean literally having a negative number of lost customers.

Operational safeguards, not extra claimed book exercises: price changes and trial billing must be communicated transparently; follow applicable consent and contact rules for outreach; protect customer data. Defensibility through workflow value is not a recommendation to obstruct cancellation or data access. Funding, employment incentives, ownership, and tax arrangements need current, situation-specific professional review.
