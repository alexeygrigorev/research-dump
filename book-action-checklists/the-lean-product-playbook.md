# The Lean Product Playbook — Action Checklist

**Dan Olsen · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

Actions reconstructed from summaries of this specific book. The author's contents page supplies the fifteen-chapter navigation. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every instruction in the full text. [LO]

**Use:** change `- [ ]` to `- [x]` as you work. **Practice** adapts reported advice into an action; **Reflection** is a decision; **Option** is an alternative method or explicitly identified case-study adaptation. Labels and task splitting are editorial. Choose methods suited to your uncertainty; do not execute every alternative merely to finish the list.

## Part I — Core Concepts

## 1. Achieving Product-Market Fit with the Lean Product Process

- [ ] **Practice:** Describe the customer need your product should satisfy better than alternatives. [LF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record hypotheses for customers, needs, value proposition, features, and user experience. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify which pyramid layer currently has the weakest supporting evidence. [LS]

## 2. Problem Space versus Solution Space

- [ ] **Practice:** Separate desired customer outcomes from proposed product implementations. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Investigate how people currently solve the problem before proposing your solution. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Translate a requested feature into the underlying need it expresses. [LJ]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether your own product usage represents your intended customers. [LJ]

## Part II — The Lean Product Process

## 3. Determine Your Target Customers — Step 1

- [ ] **Practice:** List plausible customer segments before choosing one to investigate. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish the person using the product from the person purchasing it. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare segments by needs, behavior, attitudes, and relevant demographic or business characteristics. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Interview people from the intended segment rather than convenient substitutes. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Create a realistic persona describing goals, context, frustrations, and existing behavior. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether early enthusiasts differ from the mainstream customers you hope to reach. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Revise the persona when research contradicts the initial picture. [LS]

## 4. Identify Underserved Customer Needs — Step 2

- [ ] **Practice:** List outcomes customers want without embedding a particular solution. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask why an outcome matters until its practical value becomes clear. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Write benefits as improvements customers can recognize, not internal technical achievements. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Assess each need's importance and satisfaction with existing alternatives. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prioritize important needs that existing options satisfy poorly. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use Kano to distinguish necessities, performance improvements, and potential delights. [LS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether an exciting differentiator leaves a basic customer expectation unmet. [LS]

## 5. Define Your Value Proposition — Step 3

- [ ] **Practice:** Compare alternatives against the same customer benefits in a value-proposition grid. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Include manual work or nonconsumption when these are genuine alternatives. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose the benefits on which your product should outperform. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** State what you will deliberately not prioritize. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether the proposed advantage matters to your chosen segment. [LS]

## 6. Specify Your Minimum Viable Product Feature Set — Step 4

- [ ] **Practice:** Brainstorm alternative features for each benefit before choosing an implementation. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Express features as user stories connecting a user, action, and benefit. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check stories for independence, negotiability, value, estimability, small size, and testability: INVEST. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Break large features into smaller, useful increments. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare expected customer value with implementation effort. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Select a coherent first release containing necessities and a meaningful advantage. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Defer work that does not test or deliver that initial proposition. [LF]

## 7. Create Your MVP Prototype — Step 5

- [ ] **Practice:** Specify the hypothesis each prototype is intended to test. [LJ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose the least expensive fidelity that can answer your research question. [LF]
- [ ] **Option:** Start with sketches or wireframes to explore structure. [LJ]
- [ ] **Option:** Use interactive mockups when the interaction itself needs testing. [LJ]
- [ ] **Option:** Test interest through a landing page or explanatory video. [LJ]
- [ ] **Option:** Deliver the experience manually through a concierge or Wizard-of-Oz test. [LJ]
- [ ] **Option:** Use a five-second exposure to investigate first impressions and comprehension. [LJ]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish evidence of interest from evidence that customers can use the product. [LC]

## 8. Apply the Principles of Great User Experience Design

- [ ] **Practice:** Work through conceptual design, information architecture, interaction, and visual presentation. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Map the main user flow before polishing individual screens. [LC]
- [ ] **Option:** Use card sorting to investigate how customers group information. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make important features discoverable rather than merely present. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check visual hierarchy by squinting at or blurring the interface. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use whitespace to distinguish groups and emphasize important information. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review interface wording as part of usability, not as decoration. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Consider mobile and larger-screen experiences together where both matter. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Design feedback, errors, and transitions—not only the ideal success screen. [LC]

## 9. Test Your MVP with Customers — Step 6

- [ ] **Practice:** Recruit target users with a screener that checks relevant characteristics. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Plan small waves of individual sessions, rather than one large final test. [LS]
- [ ] **Option:** Begin with the book's suggested five-to-eight-person qualitative testing wave. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose remote or in-person testing to suit the prototype and research question. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prepare a discussion guide covering context, realistic tasks, and closing questions. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Schedule recurring test sessions so research is not indefinitely postponed. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain that criticism is useful and the product—not the participant—is being tested. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask participants to think aloud while attempting the tasks. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Observe without rescuing participants or answering your own questions. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Let colleagues observe firsthand instead of relying only on your interpretation. [LP]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record feedback separately about value, functionality, usability, and messaging. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish difficulty using the solution from lack of interest in its benefit. [LC]

## 10. Iterate and Pivot to Improve Product-Market Fit

- [ ] **Practice:** Turn test findings into revised hypotheses rather than a miscellaneous feature list. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Address the lowest unsupported pyramid layer before refining higher layers. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Run another hypothesis–design–test–learn cycle. [LS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Verify that previously observed problems disappear after changes. [LP]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Consider a different customer, need, or proposition when repeated tests reject the current one. [LS]

## Part III — Building and Optimizing Your Product

## 12. Build Your Product Using Agile Development

- [ ] **Practice:** Deliver working increments instead of delaying all feedback until completion. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep a prioritized backlog connected to customer value. [LB]
- [ ] **Option:** Use Scrum with planning, short iterations, reviews, and retrospectives. [LB]
- [ ] **Option:** Use Kanban to visualize flow and limit work in progress. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Break development work into small pieces that can be estimated and tested. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Involve product, design, engineering, and quality assurance in shared decisions. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Automate repeatable tests and integrate changes regularly. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Improve the delivery process using feedback about completed work. [LB]

## 13. Measure Your Key Metrics

- [ ] **Practice:** Define acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, and referral for this product. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Instrument the events needed to measure those definitions. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare retention cohorts rather than relying on cumulative user totals. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish actionable conversion measures from attractive but uninformative totals. [LB]
- [ ] **Option:** Use the product-disappointment survey as one signal of fit, not a verdict. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Combine behavioral numbers with interviews that explain the behavior. [LB]

## 14. Use Analytics to Optimize Your Product and Business

- [ ] **Practice:** Establish the current baseline before changing the product. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Select the most important metric or bottleneck to improve next. [LH]
- [ ] **Practice:** Rank improvement ideas by likely impact and effort. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Form a testable prediction and compare results after the change. [LC]
- [ ] **Practice:** Address retention and conversion weaknesses before simply purchasing more traffic. [LS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Consider a larger change when small optimizations reach diminishing returns. [LC]

## 15. Conclusion

- [ ] **Practice:** Keep assumptions explicit as the product and market evolve. [LB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Continue customer learning after launch rather than declaring product-market fit permanently finished. [LF]

## Interpretation and coverage

Chapter names follow the official contents; some capitalization is normalized. Bookey labels introductory sections as extra chapters, so its numbering is not used here. Its quizzes, imagined examples, and reading recommendations are excluded. The case-study actions are adaptations, not promises of the same outcome.

A five-to-eight-person test is a qualitative starting point, not a guarantee of finding every issue or a statistically representative sample. Survey thresholds are heuristics, not proof of product-market fit. The book's discussion of Agile is summarized historically, not presented as the latest official Scrum specification.

Implementation safeguards, not additional claimed book exercises: obtain permission before recording research, protect participant data, disclose what a prototype or presale actually provides, and do not charge for something represented as already available when it is not.
