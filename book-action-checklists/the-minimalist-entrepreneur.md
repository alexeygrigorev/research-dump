# The Minimalist Entrepreneur — Action Checklist

**Sahil Lavingia · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

Actions reconstructed from summaries of this specific book, with selected points corroborated by the author's book interview. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every instruction in the original. The eight chapter headings follow the summary's book navigation. [MB]

**Use:** mark finished actions with `- [x]`. **Practice** adapts a recommendation into a task; **Reflection** is a decision; **Option** is an alternative. Labels and task splitting are editorial. A public launch, advertising, hiring, and outside funding are not mandatory milestones for every business.

## 1. The Minimalist Entrepreneur

- [ ] **Reflection:** Define the livelihood and freedom you want the business to support. [MF]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide whether you want a lasting business rather than primarily a future sale. [MF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Create and share something small instead of waiting to feel entrepreneurial. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Learn the business skills needed for your next actual problem. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Design for paying customers and sustainable margins from the beginning. [MZ]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Compare outside funding's obligations with the independence you want to preserve. [MI]
- [ ] **Practice:** Judge progress by useful paid work, not only attention or apparent scale. [MA]

## 2. Start with Community

- [ ] **Practice:** List communities where you already spend time or genuinely want to participate. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Include professional groups, interests, and offline communities—not only startup circles. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Observe recurring conversations before arriving with a sales pitch. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Move from reading to contributing useful answers and sharing your work. [MR]
- [ ] **Option:** Teach something useful or help organize a relevant community activity. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Notice recurring complaints and burdensome existing workarounds. [MZ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Speak with members about problems consuming their time or money. [MZ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether solving the problem fits your interests and abilities. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether the community contains enough potential paying customers for your goals. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose a problem with an understandable route to payment. [MR]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Ask whether you would remain interested in these people without an immediate sale. [MO]

## 3. Build as Little as Possible

- [ ] **Practice:** Solve one specific problem for one person before designing for thousands. [MZ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Start with a manual valuable process where possible. [MS]
- [ ] **Option:** Deliver through a call or spreadsheet before building custom software. [MI]
- [ ] **Practice:** Document the steps that produced the customer's result. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Repeat and refine the process using customer feedback. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify what another person would need to reproduce the result. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Automate only after understanding the process being automated. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prioritize automation that removes recurring effort or mistakes. [MS]
- [ ] **Option:** Use existing tools instead of writing every component yourself. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Limit the first product to the essential proven workflow. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Release improvements in small steps and learn from actual use. [MR]
- [ ] **Option:** Support yourself through services while learning what can become a product. [MI]

### Put only the necessary infrastructure in place

- [ ] **Practice:** Choose a name people can remember, spell, and find. [MF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Create a simple online place explaining the offer. [MF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Set up business email and relevant public profiles. [MF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Provide a working way to accept payment. [MF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give customers an easy way to send feedback. [MF]

## 4. Sell to Your First Hundred Customers

- [ ] **Practice:** Charge for the offer rather than treating free usage as payment evidence. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Estimate delivery costs before choosing an initial price. [MZ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Compare that price with the value customers receive. [MZ]
- [ ] **Option:** Test different packages or tiers when customers have meaningfully different needs. [MZ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Begin with relevant people you know who could genuinely benefit. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Approach community members individually for a candid conversation. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain how the offer helps instead of pressuring someone to buy. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask about their problem before defending your solution. [MA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Learn why people decline and revise the offer where warranted. [MS]
- [ ] **Option:** Contact suitable strangers through personalized outreach after your warm network. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep selling one customer at a time while learning their needs. [MA]
- [ ] **Practice:** Work toward the first hundred paying customers before relying on a launch event. [MR]
- [ ] **Option:** Use a public launch to celebrate demonstrated demand rather than manufacture it. [MR]

## 5. Market by Being You

- [ ] **Practice:** Choose places where your intended audience already pays attention. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Share what you are making and learning, not only finished successes. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Include relevant mistakes and uncertainty instead of presenting a flawless founder story. [MO]
- [ ] **Option:** Publish something that teaches a useful skill. [MO]
- [ ] **Option:** Share an experience that encourages people pursuing similar work. [MO]
- [ ] **Option:** Use entertainment when it genuinely suits your voice and audience. [MO]
- [ ] **Practice:** Reply to people and develop relationships beyond broadcasting. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Connect public content to a clear explanation of the paid offer. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Create an email signup so interested people can hear from you again. [MB]
- [ ] **Option:** Offer a useful resource as a reason to subscribe. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Continue useful communication between launches. [MS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Establish what works organically before depending on advertising spending. [MR]

## 6. Grow Yourself and Your Business Mindfully

- [ ] **Practice:** Keep recurring expenses below sustainable income. [MF]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish fixed commitments from costs that rise with actual business. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review whether each new expense improves customer value or operating capacity. [MZ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Avoid office space or other overhead bought mainly to appear established. [MZ]
- [ ] **Practice:** Plan a sustainable personal income instead of ignoring your own needs. [MB]
- [ ] **Option:** Use a specialist contractor before making an unnecessary permanent hire. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep listening to customers as revenue grows. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Discuss expectations and difficulties openly with your cofounder, where applicable. [MR]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether the next growth target is worth its demands on your energy. [MR]

## 7. Build the House You Want to Live In

- [ ] **Practice:** Define values that actually guide difficult choices. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain those values with concrete stories and examples. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Communicate how, when, and where your company works before hiring. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Write a truthful role description, including the environment and expectations. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Hire people capable of owning work you currently perform. [MI]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give people responsibilities that suit their strengths. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make important decisions understandable through written context. [MI]
- [ ] **Option:** Use asynchronous discussion when it produces more thoughtful participation. [MI]
- [ ] **Option:** Consider remote collaboration rather than limiting every role geographically. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Document work so future colleagues can operate without constant founder intervention. [MI]
- [ ] **Practice:** Set clear performance expectations and give timely feedback. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Address a persistent mismatch honestly rather than leaving it unresolved indefinitely. [MB]

## 8. Where Do We Go from Here?

- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide what you want to do with the time the business frees. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Preserve interests and relationships outside your company's work. [MI]
- [ ] **Option:** Help another creator through useful advice, introductions, or sharing what you learned. [MS]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Revisit whether the business still serves your life instead of becoming its entire purpose. [MI]

## Interpretation and coverage

This list follows the community → manual solution → paid customers → audience → sustainable organization sequence reported across the sources. “First hundred customers” is a book milestone, not a promise that a particular launch will succeed. Infrastructure tasks are practical adaptations of the public summary, not a verbatim official checklist.

Several Bookey chapters are truncated. The reader summaries and author interview fill out overlapping topics; they do not establish exhaustive original-text coverage. Reviewer anecdotes, separate editorial exercises, fabricated-sounding quotations, claims of guaranteed success, and advice imported from other books are excluded. Later tools and specific software brands are not treated as mandatory book recommendations.

The interview supplies author context for manual delivery, written collaboration, and founder independence; it is not presented as an extract from the book. Profitability does not guarantee indefinite survival. Hiring, funding, and billing choices need to fit the actual business.

Implementation safeguards, not additional claimed book exercises: use permission-based email, respect outreach refusals, describe manual delivery honestly, and handle employment or investment arrangements with appropriate professional advice.
