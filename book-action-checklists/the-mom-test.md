# The Mom Test — Action Checklist

**Rob Fitzpatrick · English reading/listening companion · Source-linked, summary-based edition**

Actions reconstructed from multiple summaries of this specific book. This is not an official workbook or a verified inventory of every instruction in the original. The eight numbered chapters follow the published sequence; the conclusion and cheatsheet are not counted as chapter 9. [MR]

**Use:** mark completed actions with `- [x]`. **Practice** adapts advice into an action; **Reflection** is a decision; **Option** is an alternative. Labels and task splitting are editorial. Choose the questions relevant to your uncertainty rather than reading every item as an interview script.

## 1. The Mom Test

### Rewrite the questions before using them

- [ ] **Practice:** Replace requests to judge your idea with questions about the customer's life. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Replace hypothetical future behavior with a specific past experience. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Let the customer speak more than you do. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Remove wording that suggests the answer you hope to hear. [MR]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Decide what concrete fact the conversation needs to uncover. [MB]

### Investigate an actual problem

- [ ] **Practice:** Ask about the most recent occurrence of the problem. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Have the customer explain what happened and how they handled it. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask which part was especially difficult. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explore the other approaches they already tried. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what they have actually paid for related solutions. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Investigate their goals, constraints, and current workarounds. [MW]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Keep responsibility for solution design instead of asking customers to design it. [MW]

## 2. Avoiding Bad Data

### Deflect compliments and anchor vague answers

- [ ] **Practice:** Mark compliments as encouragement, not evidence of demand. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Redirect praise toward a specific experience in the customer's past. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Replace claims about usual behavior with the latest actual example. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Replace future promises with evidence of action already taken. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Stop describing your sacrifices or enthusiasm to invite reassurance. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** When you start pitching, return to questions about the customer's situation. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Accept that an issue may not matter instead of arguing that it should. [ML]

### Dig beneath feature requests

- [ ] **Practice:** Ask what the requested feature would enable. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask how the person currently manages without it. [MK]
- [ ] **Practice:** Investigate why earlier attempts have not solved the problem. [MK]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask how the proposed capability would fit their day. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explore whether it is essential now or could wait. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Follow strong emotion with questions about the event and consequences behind it. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record the underlying need before adding a feature to the roadmap. [MK]

## 3. Asking Important Questions

### Expose the assumptions that could change your decision

- [ ] **Reflection:** Imagine the business failing and identify the assumption responsible. [ML]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Imagine it succeeding and identify what must be true. [ML]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Ask what advice you would give another founder in this situation. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose a question whose answer could overturn your current plan. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask the uncomfortable question rather than collecting harmless positive feedback. [MK]
- [ ] **Practice:** Begin with broader priorities before concentrating on your preferred problem. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Investigate indifference instead of searching for praise. [MN]

### Maintain the three learning priorities

- [ ] **Practice:** Write the three most important things to learn before the conversation. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Prepare separate priorities for each customer or partner type. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Update the list as answers arrive; retire resolved questions. [MK]

### Separate market risk from product risk

- [ ] **Reflection:** Distinguish uncertainty about demand from uncertainty about delivering the solution. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify assumptions that conversations alone cannot establish. [MW]
- [ ] **Option:** Use a prototype or practical experiment for unresolved product risks. [MW]

## 4. Keeping It Casual

- [ ] **Practice:** Use a brief natural conversation when a formal meeting is unnecessary. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Start with a relevant question rather than a presentation. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Share only context that helps the discussion. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Match the conversation's length to what you need to learn. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep scheduled conversations conversational rather than interrogative. [MB]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Check whether meeting preparation is delaying an answer you could obtain now. [MS]

## 5. Commitment and Advancement

*These actions apply when moving from understanding problems to discussing a concrete offer. A discovery conversation can be useful without ending in a purchase.* [MR]

### Replace friendly interest with a meaningful next step

- [ ] **Practice:** Decide which next step would genuinely advance this potential sale. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask for that step instead of ending with general enthusiasm. [MK]
- [ ] **Practice:** Assess what the person is committing: time, reputation, or money. [MS]
- [ ] **Option:** Arrange a substantive prototype review or working trial. [MN]
- [ ] **Option:** Request an introduction to relevant colleagues or a decision-maker. [MN]
- [ ] **Option:** Seek a deposit or preorder when the offer is ready for one. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Agree on a concrete next action and date. [MK]
- [ ] **Practice:** Give prospects a clear opportunity to decline. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish friendly contacts from progressing sales leads. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether a free trial requires meaningful participation. [MS]

### Recognize promising early customers

- [ ] **Practice:** Look for people who recognize a serious, current problem. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Check whether they have already searched, spent money, or improvised a workaround. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Establish whether they can commit resources to solving it. [MS]

## 6. Finding Conversations

### Go where suitable people already are

- [ ] **Option:** Use targeted cold outreach to begin conversations when introductions are unavailable. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask helpful contacts to introduce another relevant person. [ML]
- [ ] **Option:** Attend events and communities where your intended customers gather. [ML]
- [ ] **Option:** Contact interested landing-page subscribers for individual conversations. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Use relevant spontaneous encounters without waiting for a scheduled interview. [ML]

### Give people a useful reason to come to you

- [ ] **Option:** Host a meetup or knowledge-exchange discussion for the target group. [MK]
- [ ] **Option:** Teach or speak about a subject your prospective customers value. [MN]
- [ ] **Option:** Publish useful material for the industry you are investigating. [MN]
- [ ] **Option:** Ask industry advisers or university contacts for relevant introductions. [MN]

### Make a focused meeting request

- [ ] **Practice:** State the change you hope to help bring about: the vision. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain your current stage without presenting a finished solution: the framing. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify what you still need to understand: the weakness. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Explain why this person's experience is relevant: the pedestal. [ML]
- [ ] **Practice:** Make a specific, manageable request for their time: the ask. [ML]

## 7. Choosing Your Customers

### Slice the audience until its needs become coherent

- [ ] **Practice:** Identify who within a broad market most strongly needs the outcome. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask which motivation separates that subgroup from everyone else. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Separate groups with different goals, workflows, or buying conditions. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Define both the specific customer and where to reach them. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Narrow again when you cannot identify a practical place to find customers. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Revisit segmentation when interviews produce incompatible problems and priorities. [MK]
- [ ] **Practice:** Choose representative customers, not merely impressive names. [MN]

### Understand the buying group

- [ ] **Practice:** Distinguish the user from the person controlling the budget. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Ask which budget would fund the purchase. [MW]
- [ ] **Practice:** Identify other people who must approve or could block adoption. [MW]

## 8. Running the Process

### Before the conversation

- [ ] **Practice:** Review assumptions and learning priorities with the founding team. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Research basic facts about the person and industry first. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Invite core decision-makers into some customer conversations rather than filtering everything yourself. [MF]
- [ ] **Option:** Attend with one colleague so one person can concentrate on notes. [MB]

### During and immediately afterward

- [ ] **Practice:** Capture concrete observations instead of only your conclusions. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record important customer wording accurately. [MS]
- [ ] **Option:** Use consistent note symbols for emotions and important information. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Record commitments, introductions, and follow-up actions. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Complete missing notes promptly while the conversation remains fresh. [MK]
- [ ] **Practice:** Share notes promptly with product decision-makers. [MN]

### Review, decide, and continue

- [ ] **Practice:** Review evidence together rather than relying on “the customer said so.” [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Discuss which assumptions changed and what remains uncertain. [MB]
- [ ] **Practice:** Turn the review into a product decision or next learning step. [MR]
- [ ] **Practice:** Store notes where the team can retrieve and compare them. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Keep the record sortable and backed up. [MN]
- [ ] **Practice:** Review interviewing mistakes and improve the next conversation. [MB]
- [ ] **Reflection:** Stop repeating research once it no longer changes the next decision. [MS]
- [ ] **Practice:** Continue customer contact while building rather than treating research as a one-off phase. [MB]

## Conclusion and Cheatsheet

The practical loop is already represented above: choose a customer slice and three learning priorities, investigate actual behavior, seek appropriate advancement when discussing an offer, then review and act. This section adds no duplicate completion tasks.

## Coverage and interpretation notes

This covers all eight main chapters through accessible summaries, not every original example, script, or note-taking symbol. The question prompts are paraphrased adaptations rather than a reproduced official questionnaire. Sources that reorganize the material into stages are mapped back to the book's chapter sequence.

Keep three distinctions intact: **praise is not purchase evidence; a feature request is not yet a validated need; and commitment is not identical to revenue**. A letter of intent does not automatically mean money has changed hands. None of these practices guarantees truthful responses, representative sampling, technical feasibility, or product-market fit.

The three-question preparation method is explicitly reported in the book summaries. No fixed interview quota, validation deadline, or universal conversion threshold has been added. Optional recruitment methods are alternatives; the book does not require an event, blog, adviser, and landing page before your first conversation.

Reviewer-only reflections, publisher-created exercises, and advice imported from other books are excluded. SoBrief's separate “Analysis” paragraphs are not treated as Fitzpatrick's instructions. Shortform's extra material from other authors is excluded. Bookey's quizzes and publisher-written illustrative scenes are excluded.

Implementation safeguards—not additional book exercises: respect refusals and contact preferences, obtain consent before recording, protect confidential information, and make presale terms accurate. Keeping an early conversation casual does not mean disguising your identity or misrepresenting its purpose.
