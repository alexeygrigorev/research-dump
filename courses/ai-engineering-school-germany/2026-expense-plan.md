# 2026 expense plan for an April 2027 course launch

**Research date:** 9 November 2026  
**Scope:** A German sole proprietor preparing a new education programme as an addition to an existing business.  
**Status (updated 9 November 2026):** Owner explicitly confirmed **Bilanzierung**, not EÜR. VAT treatment, balance-sheet year-end details and contracts are not yet established. This is planning guidance for review with a Steuerberater.

## Decision in one paragraph

**Confirmed Bilanzierung:** Paying in December 2026 for a supplier's services to be carried out in 2027 generally does **not** make the payment an expense of 2026. Depending on the actual contract it is normally a **geleistete Anzahlung** (asset) or **aktiver Rechnungsabgrenzungsposten** (aRAP). To recognize ordinary operating expenses in 2026, prioritize actual, economically attributable 2026 professional work (e.g., legal assessment, completed curriculum editing, real certification preparation steps, market research) with evidence of the work. A 2026 service normally does not require cash payment before year-end to belong in that year's Bilanz result. Assets requiring capitalization are a separate question. A narrow §5(5) EStG tax-balance-sheet waiver for qualifying individual transitory amounts up to **€800** and a possible **§7g IAB** for qualifying future movable depreciable business assets must be assessed separately; neither makes arbitrary 2027 service prepayments a 2026 expense. Demand validation should still precede irreversible expenditure. [S1–S4]

The aim is to obtain useful work and a viable programme while making appropriate use of the applicable timing rules. Spending the entire available budget is not a success criterion.


## Confirmed approach: Bilanz, payment versus expense (9 November 2026)

The owner has confirmed **Bilanzierung** and expressly wishes to **pay providers before 31 December 2026**. This is possible commercially, but the tax timing is not controlled merely by the payment date. Preserve the ability to prepay while recognising that the deduction may fall in 2027. Do not promise a 2026 reduction from payments for services still wholly outstanding on the balance-sheet date.

| Actual 2026 transaction | Usually shown at 31 December 2026 | Effect on 2026 profit |
|---|---|---|
| Legal specialist completes a scoped assessment in December; invoiced/paid in December or January | Operating expense and payment or liability | Generally expense in 2026, subject to any requirement to capitalize a resulting asset |
| Scoped curriculum review/translation or market research is performed and delivered in December | Operating expense and payment or liability | Generally expense in 2026 if an ordinary service rather than acquisition/creation of a capitalizable asset |
| Pay a €6,000 advance in December for an AZAV audit or service wholly to be performed in February | Geleistete Anzahlung or other asset, depending on agreement | Generally **no 2026 expense** merely from the payment |
| Pay a 2027 annual subscription in December | Usually active Rechnungsabgrenzungsposten (aRAP) for 2027 | Generally **2027** expense; examine the narrow small-item tax waiver below |
| Buy a needed, qualifying computer and actually acquire it in December | Business asset; depreciation assessed under the applicable computer guidance | Potential full 2026 acquisition-year depreciation where conditions are met |
| Pay an advance in December for equipment first acquired in February | Advance asset until acquisition | No 2026 depreciation from payment alone; assess §7g IAB separately |
| Transfer money to a future GmbH or label a reserve “school development” | Financial asset or cash allocation | No ordinary 2026 operating expense |

### Narrow tax-election exception for small aRAP items

Under **§5(5) sentence 2 EStG**, a separate item that otherwise qualifies for a transitory accrual item may be left without that item **if its amount does not exceed the threshold in §6(2) sentence 1 EStG (currently €800)**. The election must be made consistently for all corresponding amounts. This applies to qualifying **Rechnungsabgrenzungsposten**, **not** automatically to a supplier's unfinished-work advance or security deposit; nor should it be used by artificially splitting one obligation into multiple small invoices. The Handelsbilanz and tax-balance-sheet effects may differ; have the Steuerberater check the actual accounts, contracts and election. [S4, S12, S13]

### Separate option: IAB under §7g

§7g explicitly permits the **Bilanz** and **EÜR** methods, subject to the legal conditions. If the appropriate 2026 business profit does not exceed €200,000 and there is a genuine plan for eligible **movable depreciable fixed assets** with near-exclusive business use (or qualifying rental), a deduction of up to **50% of the expected acquisition cost** may be available for 2026 **without paying for those assets in 2026**. Subsequent acquisition and adjustment/reversal requirements apply. This is **not** a budget for AZAV fees, marketing, rent, SaaS services, course licences or startup shares. Verify future purchase dates and §7g eligibility with the adviser before claiming an amount. [S8]

### Year-end instruction for the accountant

For each planned supplier, distinguish the value **actually received by 31 December** from the cash sent in December and the remaining future service obligation. Ask: “Is there a 2026 operating expense, a capitalizable asset, a geleistete Anzahlung or an aRAP; is there a defensible §5(5) election; and is there separately eligible §7g equipment?” Keep acceptance records, work products and actual service dates. The internal preferred payment cutoff of 18 December is **not** an accounting deadline.

## 1. Accounting-method comparison (Bilanz confirmed; EÜR retained only as background)

**Confirmed:** the business uses Bilanzierung. The following EÜR column is kept only to explain why the earlier cash-payment advice no longer controls this project. Confirm the year-end date and whether any specific expense belongs to a different legal recipient.

| Question | If the business uses EÜR | If the business uses Bilanz |
|---|---|---|
| What usually determines the timing of an ordinary service expense? | The actual outflow of funds, subject to statutory exceptions. | Economic allocation and the relevant recognition rules. |
| Does a December invoice alone establish a 2026 deduction? | Generally no: an unpaid invoice usually does not establish a cash expense. | Invoice date alone is still not decisive; performance, liabilities and allocation matter. |
| Can a genuine 2026 advance for an ordinary 2027 service affect 2026 profit? | Potentially yes. BMF expressly applies the cash rules to advances and partial payments. | Usually not simply because it has been paid: an advance or prepaid expense may remain an asset. |
| Is a December equipment purchase automatically a full deduction? | No. Asset and depreciation rules still apply. | No. Asset and depreciation rules still apply. |

Sources: §4(3), §11 and §5 EStG; BMF EStH 2025, R4.5(2). The balance-sheet rules include limited exceptions, including an optional small-item rule for certain prepaid expenses. Do not assume the general comparison resolves a particular invoice. [S1–S4]

### What a genuine advance should look like

For a proposed advance for an audit, professional review or other ordinary service, retain:

- An actual supplier and a sufficiently clear service agreement.
- A description of the business purpose, scope, expected delivery and price.
- The invoice or advance-payment request and payment evidence.
- The cancellation/refund terms and any subsequent credit note.
- Evidence of progress or delivery when available.

A **service advance** and a **security deposit (Kaution)** are different arrangements. A security deposit should not be treated as an ordinary current service expense merely because cash has moved. Conversely, a normal service advance does not automatically become a security deposit just because the contract provides a refund if the service is cancelled. Classify the actual arrangement, rather than relying on the word “deposit” in an English-language invoice.

The cash principle does not convert a purchase of an asset into an ordinary expense. Artificial invoices, backdating and transactions without a real commercial purpose are outside this plan. §11 explicitly preserves the application of §42 AO. [S1–S3, S11]

A specific exception applies to advance payments for a **Nutzungsüberlassung covering more than five years**: §11(2) requires allocation across the relevant period. This concerns that statutory category of usage payment; it is not a rule that every future service advance must be spread over its delivery period. [S2]

## 3. Equipment: distinguish three mechanisms

### Qualifying computer hardware and software

The BMF guidance of 22 February 2022 allows a useful life of one year for the specified categories of computer hardware, peripherals and operating/application software. It also accepts full depreciation in the year of acquisition or production. This remains depreciation under §7 EStG, and the required asset records still matter. [S7]

For a 2026 deduction, distinguish **acquiring a qualifying item in 2026** from **paying a 2026 advance for an item only acquired in 2027**. The latter does not by itself start depreciation. Business/private use must also be reflected.

### Low-value assets: GWG

§6(2) provides an immediate-deduction option for qualifying independently usable movable depreciable fixed assets at the statutory **€800 threshold**. The threshold is tested excluding the VAT contained in the price; the amount actually treated as tax cost also depends on input-VAT deductibility. Items above €250 require the specified records unless the information is already evident from the bookkeeping. [S5]

Do not assume every component or accessory is independently usable. The computer guidance and GWG are separate routes; the accountant should select and document the relevant treatment.

### Other long-lived assets

Ordinary depreciation applies unless another rule is available. The acquisition year normally requires a time-based calculation. Buying something in December does not universally permit its full price to be deducted in December. [S6]

## 4. Optional IAB review: only for real planned assets

An **Investitionsabzugsbetrag (IAB)** under §7g is not a general reserve for starting a course. Subject to its conditions, it can permit an earlier profit deduction of up to **50% of the anticipated acquisition/production cost of qualifying movable depreciable fixed assets**. The relevant eligibility ceiling is **profit of no more than €200,000**, calculated as specified in the provision, not turnover. [S8]

For an ordinary calendar-year 2026 deduction, the general three-following-business-year investment window ends on **31 December 2029**. Business-use and other conditions must also be met; failure can require reversal. [S8]

Ask about this only when there is a genuine, useful equipment plan. It does not provide a reserve for future teaching fees, marketing, certification services, ordinary subscriptions or shares. Where the platform and equipment already exist, it may be irrelevant to the first cohort.

## 5. VAT and ownership must be decided separately

### VAT

Income-tax deductibility and recovery of input VAT are separate questions. For an advance, §15 UStG requires, among other conditions, the relevant invoice and payment. It also restricts input-VAT deduction for costs used in exempt outputs. [S10]

If the educational programme is VAT-exempt, related input VAT may be unrecoverable. If the business has both taxable existing activities and exempt education, allocation of shared inputs may be necessary. Do not build the budget on an assumption that every supplier's VAT will be refunded. Establish the programme's actual VAT treatment with the accountant.

### Existing sole proprietorship versus a future GmbH

Keep the actual recipient and owner of each service or asset clear. If the first programme is an expansion of the existing sole proprietorship, contracts and records should accurately reflect that fact. Costs belonging to the formation or activities of a separate GmbH cannot automatically be attributed to the sole proprietorship. A capital contribution itself is not an ordinary current operating expense. [S1]

If assets, intellectual property or the course business are later transferred to a GmbH, review and document that transaction separately. Do not create a GmbH solely to make a payment look deductible.

Do not assume that a last-day payment instruction will resolve every timing question. Arrange important payments with a buffer and keep evidence of the actual outflow. A December invoice paid in January is not automatically a December EÜR expense. [S2, S9]

### The ten-day rule is narrow

Regularly recurring expenses paid around year-end may be allocated differently from the general cash rule. Case law requires both payment and due date within the relevant short period, generally ten days, along with the other conditions. This is not a general extension allowing arbitrary January supplier payments to be deducted in the previous year. VAT advance payments can be affected, and an extension of the VAT due date can change the result. [S9]

## 7. Optional budget envelope after demand validation

An illustrative **€5,000–€10,000 cumulative commitment ceiling** can be used as a management decision envelope **after** the demand gate has been met. This is not a recommendation to spend that amount, a certification-price estimate, a supplier quotation or a statement about anyone's available funds.

Use the envelope as follows:

1. Identify the next evidence-backed milestone and what cannot reasonably be done internally.
2. Obtain an actual written offer for that specific need.
3. Classify its accounting/VAT treatment before payment.
4. Commit only if the deliverable is useful at its full after-tax cost.
5. Keep unused funds unspent and retain working capital for the first cohort.

If useful external work costs less, spend less. If required offers exceed the envelope, make a new operating decision based on validated demand and delivery economics; do not assume the higher total is justified by a deduction.

For illustration only: at a hypothetical 40% marginal tax rate, a fully deductible €1,000 expense reduces tax by approximately €400 and still costs approximately €600 after that tax effect. This is arithmetic, not an estimate of an individual's tax position, and ignores VAT and other effects. A deduction does not reimburse the purchase price.
