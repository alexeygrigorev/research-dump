# Positioning and messaging for the AI Engineering School

**13 November 2026. Draft for review.** A rewrite of how we talk about the school, following [Oversubscribed](principles.md). It replaces the tone of the [outreach drafts](../materials/outreach.md), which were written to research demand ("I'm exploring", "take a survey"). Ready copy is in the [announcement proposal](2026-11-13-announcement-proposal.md).

## The position in one paragraph

A small, in-person AI engineering school in Berlin, taught by Alexey Grigorev himself. The first cohort has 20 seats, because one room and one instructor cannot do more. The Agentur für Arbeit can pay the tuition through a Bildungsgutschein once the course is approved, but funding does not give anyone a seat. Seats go to the people who apply, complete a practical task and fit the cohort.

## The four messages

Use them in this order. Every post, page and ad carries at least the first three.

### 1. The offer is limited

> The first cohort has 20 seats.

A number, stated early, without adjectives. Not "limited places", not "small group".

### 2. The reason is physical

> One room and one instructor. The room doesn't fit more people, and I can't review more projects properly.

This is the part that makes the limit believable. Always give both halves: the room and the attention. The numbers behind it are in the [rationale](20-seat-rationale.md).

### 3. Funding is not admission

> A Bildungsgutschein pays for a seat. It doesn't get you one.

Longer form:

> The Agentur für Arbeit decides whether it funds your training. I decide who is in the room. Those are two separate decisions, and you need both.

Readers in the Bildungsgutschein market are used to schools that take everyone with a voucher. This sentence is what sets the school apart, so it should never be dropped.

### 4. There is competition for the seats

> I expect more applications than seats, so I select the cohort. You apply, do a short practical task, and we talk for 20 minutes.

Say "I expect" until the numbers exist. Once accepted applicants exceed 20, say so with the real number.

## What we do not say

| Don't say | Because | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Free", "kostenlos", "100% funded" | Approval is pending and each voucher is an individual decision | "The Agentur für Arbeit can cover the tuition" |
| "Exclusive", "elite", "only the best" | It reads as arrogance, and selection is about fit and readiness | "I select for fit: can you code a little, can you attend, do you know why you're here" |
| "Tiny", "smallest cohort in Berlin" | Not true. Other schools run 10 to 20 | "20 seats" |
| "Seats are going fast" | Not known | A real date and, later, a real count |
| "Guaranteed job" | Cannot be promised | "You finish with a deployed project and can explain how you built and tested it" |
| "Everyone welcome" | Contradicts selection | "Career changers are welcome if they already program a bit" |
| "Sign up", "register" | Registration language | "Apply" |

## Who it is for, and who it is not for

For:

- People in Germany who already write some code.
- People who can attend full-time, Monday to Friday, for the whole course.
- People who can work in English.
- People who want an AI or software engineering job afterwards, including those between jobs.

Not for, in the first cohort:

- People who have never programmed. Point them to the free Zoomcamps first.
- People who need a part-time or evening course.
- People who want a certificate with little work.

Saying who it is not for is part of the book's principle 5, and it makes the invitation stronger for those who fit.

## Selection, described honestly

Selection here does not mean ranking people by talent. It means checking three things: prerequisites, availability and intent. The criteria are public and the same for everyone ([form proposal](2026-11-13-announcement-proposal.md#selection-criteria-to-publish-on-the-form)). Employment and benefit status are not criteria.

This matters for the public funder as well. A school that checks suitability before admitting someone is easier to defend to an adviser than one that takes everyone. Whether and how the suitability check has to be documented for the certifier is an open question in the [operations notes](../operations-and-azav.md) and should be confirmed there, not assumed.

## Voice

First person, plain, specific. Short sentences. Numbers and dates instead of adjectives. No countdown emojis, no capital-letter urgency. The same voice as the Zoomcamp announcements, with one change: this is an invitation with conditions, not an open door.

## Answers to the questions people will ask

**"If the state pays, why can't everyone join?"**
Because the state pays for the seat, not for a bigger room or a second me. There are 20 seats.

**"I already have a Bildungsgutschein. Am I in?"**
Not automatically. Apply like everyone else. Also, the course is not approved yet, and an existing voucher may not match this course. Your adviser decides that.

**"Will you open more seats if many people apply?"**
No. If there is more demand than 20, there is a waitlist and a second cohort later.

**"What if I'm accepted and the Agentur says no?"**
You can pay yourself or release the seat. The seat then goes to the next person on the waitlist.

**"How do you choose?"**
By the criteria on the application: programming experience, availability, English, a concrete goal, and the practical task.

**"Is it certified?"**
The approval is in progress. I'll say when it's granted.

## If the cohort is online

The room argument only works in person. If the first cohort runs live online, use:

> I teach this cohort myself and review every project. I can do that properly for 20 people, not for more.

Do not mention a room in copy for an online cohort.
