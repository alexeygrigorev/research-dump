# Schedule, founder capacity and existing-business integration

**Working proposal, 9 November 2026.** Times use **Europe/Berlin**, including the relevant daylight-saving changes. No calendar events have been created or moved. The month of April is the agreed target; the exact days below remain proposals.

## The capacity constraint

The proposed course is a meaningful part-time operating commitment. A first cohort of about eight participants with similar prerequisites is estimated to require:

| Work | Hours per teaching week |
|---|---:|
| Live teaching and supervised practice | 12 |
| Preparation, project review and assessment | 5–7 |
| Administration, learner support and programme management | 3–4 |
| **Total** | **20–23** |

These are planning estimates, not measured delivery data. Early weeks, audit preparation, funding questions and difficult projects can exceed them. The course cannot simply be added to an unchanged full workload. Before enrolment, allocate hours for current community management, sales, content, courses, meetings and podcasts as well as the new programme.

The proposed four-morning pattern conflicts with existing commitments and needs an explicit capacity review. A public research archive should preserve that operational conclusion without exposing personal calendar entries. Do not treat an apparently empty calendar slot as confirmed working capacity or cancel existing commitments by assumption.

## Proposed teaching morning

| Time, Monday–Thursday | Activity | Synchronous units |
|---|---|---:|
| 09:00–10:30 | Explanation, demonstration, initial work | 2 UE |
| 10:30–10:45 | Break | 0 |
| 10:45–12:15 | Supervised practice, review and discussion | 2 UE |
| After 12:15 | Lunch; separately scheduled preparation or existing-business work | Not automatically course UE |

Four mornings provide 16 UE, each 45 minutes: **12 clock hours of live teaching per week**. Learners also complete about eight clock hours of independent practice. Independent work, recordings and ordinary support messages are described separately; they do not inflate synchronous teaching hours. This follows the [BA implementation note on teaching units](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/datei/umsetzungshinweis-01-2026_ba055638.pdf).

The four morning blocks occupy **13 calendar hours including breaks**, even though they contain 12 teaching hours. Reserve the whole block when assessing availability; breaks do not create usable meeting slots.

An illustrative founder allocation is 12 live hours, four hours of afternoon preparation, three Friday-morning hours and two hours of short preparation/administration: 21 course hours. This is a workload illustration, not a claim that all those slots are currently free. If only five hours remain for the existing business, the plan is incomplete until that business's actual needs are budgeted.

## Protect the rest of the business

1. List current obligations and their full weekly effort, including preparation and follow-up.
2. Identify which commitments actually end before April, which can be reduced, and which must continue.
3. Reserve course teaching and administration hours only after making those decisions.
4. Keep new teaching and meetings ending by 16:00. Existing later commitments require their own changes and counterpart agreement.
5. Reserve a specific podcast production/interview window, for example a suitable Tuesday afternoon, after checking availability. The instructor cannot record a podcast while simultaneously claiming to supervise a live funded lesson.
6. If 20–23 hours cannot be freed, change the course volume or choose a smaller commercial experiment and recalculate the product and price.

Two mornings per week over twelve teaching weeks would produce 96 synchronous UE, half the current proposal. It is a different course economics and approval question, not the same product with half the instructor commitment.

The public Maven schedule is a separate source of commitments and can change. Check the [published course page](https://maven.com/alexey-grigorev/product-shipping) and actual obligations before launch planning; historical dates in other repository folders do not prove current availability.

## Reuse work with AI Shipping Labs

Choose one useful engineering case and develop it once. The core course can use it as a structured assignment with prerequisites, rubric and support. An existing [AI Shipping Labs](https://aishippinglabs.com/) event can use the same case as an optional discussion of trends, design trade-offs or lessons learned. Public notes can follow from that work.

For the first iteration, keep shared community events **optional extracurricular activities outside the 192 mandatory UE**. Do not require an extra paid membership for required learning. Optional access and expectations should be clear to participants.

If a future shared event is to count as required funded instruction, agree its learning objective, qualified supervision, attendance, access and cost allocation with the FKS in advance. One piece of preparation can serve several products, but hours and costs must reflect their actual use. Do not double-count a shared expense or count the same teaching time twice merely because two audiences attend.

## Provisional course end

Starting Monday 12 April and teaching on Monday–Thursday, omit the Berlin public holidays on **6 May and 17 May 2027**. The 48th teaching morning then falls on **Tuesday 6 July 2027**. This is twelve equivalent teaching weeks spread across a slightly longer calendar period. Berlin school summer holidays begin on **1 July**, which may affect participant and instructor availability. Source: [Berlin holiday calendar](https://www.berlin.de/tourismus/infos/1887651-1721039-feiertage-schulferien.html).

The approved calendar must state actual teaching days and breaks. Administrative support continues over the calendar span; do not assume all non-live work vanishes in a public-holiday week. Confirm availability before promising these dates.

## Graduate teaching assistants and later iterations

| Stage | Graduate role | Alexey's responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| First cohort | Student work and an optional expression of future interest | Teaching, assessment, support and administration |
| After graduation | Paid trial task or teach-back and role preparation | Select for technical independence, explanation and dependable feedback |
| Next cohort | Environment setup, common questions, initial rubric-based review, supervised explanations | Core curriculum, difficult cases and final quality decisions |
| Later cohorts | More teaching only after competence and the operating model are demonstrated | Programme management and outcome review |

Do not assume that current students can cover the founder's illness, work for free, or become lead teachers merely by graduating. Do not promise all students a job. Model the second cohort with a **paid TA**, and the later delegated model with a **fully costed lead instructor**.

GmbH and an offline venue are separate later decisions. An Einzelunternehmen can be an employer, and a new legal entity may require changes to certification and contracts. Moving offline and delegating teaching simultaneously makes it harder to identify what caused any delivery problems; prefer one material change at a time after the first cohort's evidence.
