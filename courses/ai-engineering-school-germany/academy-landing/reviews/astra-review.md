1. **Blocker — The recorded observations cannot support the promised visitor/choice tests.** Where: `plans/promo-campaign-plan.md:22,32,44,184,208`; `site/src/scripts/signup.ts:62`; `api/lambda/handler.py:124,154`.

   **Evidence:** The numerator is one record per email AND track; the denominator is every page load. Reloads count again, one person can visit or join both tracks, and there is no exposure identifier, first-choice event, home-panel click event or record of displayed panel order. Thus a track-page view cannot establish an exclusive home-page choice or even that the visitor came from home. The claim that repeat visits inflate both denominators alike is an unsupported assumption: one offer can require more consideration. Two-proportion power calculations require independent observations with a matching conversion definition; the home-page binomial calculation requires one defined choice per participant. Current aggregate exports cannot recover either. Browser automation is excluded from page views, but that is not deduplication or reliable bot filtering.

   **Concrete fix:** Define the experimental unit and conversion window before launch. Record a privacy-reviewed exposure/assignment identifier and one conversion per unit, including a policy for people interested in both tracks. Record actual home choices separately with displayed order and distinguish first choice from subsequent exploration. If preserving completely identifier-free counters, describe results as descriptive page-load ratios and do not attach the current visitor-based significance or power claims to them.

2. **Major — Recommended spend does not guarantee the advertised detectable difference.** Where: `plans/promo-campaign-plan.md:32–88`; `plans/README.md`, Budget summary.

   **Evidence:** I independently recomputed the standard normal-approximation, two-sided, 80%-power sample sizes. The seven table rows are essentially correct (some round down by one). For example, 4% versus 8% needs about 553/435 observations per arm at 95%/90% confidence; 4% versus 6% needs 1,863/1,468. But 80% power is not certainty. More importantly, the plan assumes 2–3% social conversion while advertising detection based on higher baselines: 2% versus 4% needs 1,141/899 per arm; 3% versus 6% needs 749/590. Excluding the €200 LinkedIn quality check, the channel budgets buy approximately 1,140–2,422 clicks total, or 570–1,211 per arm even assuming equal delivery and no click-to-load loss. The stated 42–88 signups and €28–60 acquisition costs do reproduce the channel assumptions; these are forecasts, not guarantees. At a 3% weaker-track baseline, detecting a 1.5 ratio needs about 1,983 per arm at 90% confidence and 80% power. The €5,000 tier also cannot guarantee that at the expensive end.

   **Concrete fix:** Replace every “for certain” claim with conditional power estimates, exclude non-comparison spend, allow for valid-visit loss, and specify a baseline/MDE pair. With the plan's channel-mix CPC range, 3% versus 4.5% requires roughly €4,000–€8,200 including the fixed €200 LinkedIn check, before visit loss, for the 90%/80% calculation. Treat €2,500 as a pilot capable of detecting some large effects, with a legitimate inconclusive outcome. Verify actual Germany/keyword forecasts before approval; the vendor CPC numbers are not specific evidence for this offer.

3. **Major — The decision rule can manufacture a winner or mislabel low power as no meaningful difference; its extension exceeds the headline budget.** Where: `plans/promo-campaign-plan.md:204–212`.

   **Evidence:** Rule 2 declares a winner on a 1.3 ratio in three sources without any minimum conversion count or calibrated error rate. Small counts such as 4 versus 3 in each source pass it. Rule 3 calls everything else “no demand difference worth acting on”; failure to reject equality is not evidence that the true difference is below 1.5. Rule 4's extra week at the same €2,500/14-day rate costs about €1,250 more, but that contingency is absent from the budget table and launch checklist. A 400-page-view minimum is also below many of the plan's own power requirements. Rule 5's 60-signup threshold depends on unspecified organic volume; the conservative paid forecast is only about 42.

   **Concrete fix:** Pre-register one primary decision procedure, including inconclusive results and zero-count handling. Label the 1.3 rule exploratory or calibrate the whole rule with simulation. Require an interval/equivalence criterion to claim absence of a meaningful difference. Specify the maximum spend (€3,750 if all channels extend), explicit extension authorization and a revised reporting date. Define the overall-demand threshold against eligible exposure volume, not an unconditioned count alone.

4. **Major — The campaign changes the ad before the click but judges mostly the people who clicked; pooling channels adds another bias.** Where: `plans/promo-campaign-plan.md:13–26,73–76,208`.

   **Evidence:** Different track ads select different subsets of viewers. Even a randomized ad experiment does not make the resulting clickers comparable: an appealing broad ad can produce more interested signups overall yet have a lower landing-page conversion rate. The plan recognizes signups per 1,000 impressions but does not use that measure in its winning rule. Equal budgets do not yield equal visitor counts or equal source mixtures. Pooling 2% Reddit traffic and 6.5% Search traffic without stratification can favor whichever arm receives more high-intent traffic. “Two ads in one ad group with even rotation” on Reddit is not evidence of randomized, non-overlapping people, and that capability is not verified. Paid traffic can also include existing followers; no warm-audience exclusion or overlap policy is specified.

   **Concrete fix:** Choose between a landing-page experiment (same neutral ad, randomized destination) and an offer-from-impression experiment (randomized ad exposure, primary end-to-end conversion measure). Predefine source-stratified analysis or common weights; keep unrandomized Reddit and promoted-post observations exploratory. Document feasible delivery settings, audience eligibility and overlap handling before launch. Geography should be explicitly identical: the Reddit row currently says Germany and Europe while other arms say Germany.

5. **Major — Attribution is not coherent first/last touch, and paid/organic LinkedIn denominators collapse together.** Where: `site/src/scripts/signup.ts:20–27,69–74`; `api/lambda/handler.py:103–114,160–164`; `api/scripts/export_signups.py:11`; `plans/promo-campaign-plan.md:166,177,183–185`.

   **Evidence:** Home-to-track forwarding works for all five UTM fields. However, page views omit `utm_medium`, while organic LinkedIn and its promoted post have the same source, campaign and empty creative; they therefore share identical page-view keys. Signups retain medium, so their paid/organic numerators cannot be paired with separate denominators. Server “first touch” is the first NONEMPTY value of EACH field at submission time, not a first-visit snapshot. Example: submit with source=x and no campaign; submit later with source=google and campaign=paid-test: first fields become source=x/campaign=paid-test, a combination that never occurred. Last fields likewise keep stale values when a later visit omits them. No pre-signup return-visit attribution is retained, and the export omits `last_utm_*`.

   **Concrete fix:** Include medium in page-view aggregation/export and give promoted posts distinct creative identifiers. Store first and last attribution as atomic snapshots, explicitly representing missing/direct values, and export the attribution used for analysis. If not tracking earlier visits, call it “first signup attribution,” not first touch. Freeze campaign/window inclusion rules so returning organic leads cannot silently enter the paid numerator.

13. **Minor — Email is truncated before validation, so some legitimate long addresses fail or are stored incorrectly.** Where: `api/lambda/handler.py:18,51–53,75–77`; `site/src/components/SignupForm.astro`, email input.

    **Evidence:** `clean()` truncates every string to 200 characters; signup then applies it to email before the nominal 254-character check. That check can never detect excessive original length. A syntactically valid address between 201 and 254 characters can lose the end of its domain and still match the permissive regex, or become invalid despite passing the browser's original-address validation. In the former case the UI would show success for a different address. This is established by code inspection; no long address was posted.

    **Concrete fix:** Validate the complete trimmed email before any normalization, reject unsupported lengths explicitly, and never truncate an address. Keep frontend/backend rules aligned and add boundary tests that assert the stored address equals the accepted input.
