### 1. Google Search ad descriptions exceed Google Ads' hard 90-character limit, and the shared keyword list + ad copy bias Search toward AI Engineering
- **Severity:** blocker
- **Where:** `courses/ai-engineering-school-germany/academy-landing/promo-campaign-plan.md:25`, `139–146`
- **What is wrong:**
  1. **Unlaunchable ad copy:** Google Responsive Search Ads (RSAs) enforce a hard limit of **90 characters per Description** (and require at least two descriptions). Both planned descriptions far exceed 90 characters and will be rejected by Google Ads on creation.
  2. **Severe track bias across €900 (36% of paid budget):** Both arms of the 50/50 Google Search experiment bid on the same single keyword list (`promo-campaign-plan.md:146`): `"KI Weiterbildung"`, `"AI Weiterbildung Berlin"`, `"AI engineering course"`, `"AI bootcamp Berlin"`, `"Weiterbildung Softwareentwicklung KI"`, `"coding agents course"`, `"LLM course"`, `"Bildungsgutschein KI Weiterbildung"`, `"Bildungsgutschein Programmieren"`. Seven of the nine keyword themes contain `"AI"` or `"KI"`, `"AI engineering course"` literally matches Track B's Headline 1 (`"AI Engineering Program"`), and `"LLM course"` matches Track B's description (`"LLM applications"`). Meanwhile, Track A's ad (`"Agentic Engineering Program"` / `"Build, test and ship software with coding agents..."`) **does not contain the word `"AI"` or `"KI"` anywhere**. Because Google Ads' Ad Rank depends on Expected CTR, Ad Relevance, and Landing Page Experience (Quality Score), Track B will systematically achieve higher Quality Scores, lower CPCs, and more volume on the dominant `"AI"`/`"KI"` queries, while routing someone who searched `"AI engineering course"` to `"Agentic Engineering"` measures keyword mismatch rather than market demand. In addition, 5 of the 9 keyword themes are in **German** while the ad copy and landing pages are 100% **English**, depressing Ad Relevance across both arms.
- **Evidence:** Exact character counts of `promo-campaign-plan.md:144`: Agentic description = **151 characters**; AI description = **143 characters** (Google Ads RSA hard limit: **90 characters**). Keyword list at `promo-campaign-plan.md:146` vs. headlines/descriptions at `140–144`.
- **Concrete fix:**
  1. Split each RSA description into two ≤90-char descriptions of parallel structure, e.g. Description 1: `"Build, test and ship software with coding agents. Full-time, live, Berlin or online."` (84 chars) vs. `"Build, evaluate and run LLM applications. Full-time, live, Berlin or online."` (76 chars); Description 2 (shared): `"Bildungsgutschein funding planned, approval pending. Join the 15-seat shortlist."` (80 chars).
  2. Restrict the shared 50/50 experiment keyword list to **track-neutral** upskilling queries (removing `"AI engineering course"`, `"LLM course"`, and `"coding agents course"`, or running track-specific intent keywords only as a separate search-volume diagnostic rather than pooling them into the head-to-head conversion comparison), include `"AI"` in a shared headline/description so Ad Relevance on `"AI"`/`"KI"` queries is equalized, and state `"In English"` in a headline/description when targeting German keywords so non-English speakers do not waste paid clicks.

### 2. Primary metric (`signups / page_views` pooled across channels) and Decision Rules #1–#2 suffer from post-click selection bias, Simpson's Paradox, and undeduplicated dual-track signups
- **Severity:** blocker
- **Where:** `courses/ai-engineering-school-germany/academy-landing/promo-campaign-plan.md:11–13`, `193–210`; `/home/alexey/git/aws-infra-wt-academy/main/academy/lambda/handler.py:124`; `/home/alexey/git/aws-infra-wt-academy/main/academy/scripts/export_signups.py:35–41`
- **What is wrong:** Three methodological flaws in the primary metric and decision rules can suppress a genuine winner or crown the track that generated far fewer signups per euro:
  1. **Post-click conditioning bias on paid and organic traffic:** In paid ads (`promo-campaign-plan.md:21, 131`), the ad headline differs by track, so clicking is already a post-treatment outcome. Comparing `signups / page_views` conditions on a post-treatment filter. For example, if both Meta arms spend €550 across 37,000 impressions and Agentic attracts broad developer interest (`3.0%` CTR → `1,000` page views → `60` signups = `6.00%` page CVR, **€9.17/signup**) while AI attracts only narrow niche clickers (`0.8%` CTR → `270` page views → `25` signups = `9.26%` page CVR, **€22.00/signup**), Decision Rule #1 (`promo-campaign-plan.md:208`) declares **AI Engineering the winner** (`9.26% / 6.00% = 1.54×`, two-sided `p = 0.057 < 0.10`) even though Agentic produced **2.4× more signups at less than half the cost per signup**. The same collider bias breaks Decision Rule #2 (`promo-campaign-plan.md:209`) on organic traffic via `/`: if 400 home-page visitors open Agentic and 40 sign up (`10%`), while 150 open AI and 21 sign up (`14%`), AI wins the `1.3×` organic `signup rate` check (`14% / 10% = 1.4×`) while losing organic signups nearly 2-to-1 (`21` vs `40`).
  2. **Simpson's Paradox when pooling across channels:** Decision Rule #1 pools `cold paid traffic of all channels together` (`line 208`), mixing Google Search (assumed `6.5%` CVR), Meta (`3.0%`), and Reddit (`2.0%`). Whenever CPC or CTR differs by track within a channel (guaranteeing unequal click shares across Search vs. Social), raw pooling biases the combined `signups / page_views` toward whichever track got a higher fraction of its clicks from Google Search—even if both tracks have identical conversion rates inside every channel.
  3. **Dual-track signups compress the ratio toward 1.0:** DynamoDB keys items by `pk = SIGNUP#<email>`, `sk = TRACK#<track>` (`handler.py:124`), and `export_signups.py` outputs one row per `(email, track)` with no deduplication across tracks. Visitors who want to join the cohort whichever track runs (and click `"Looking at the other track?"` in `Footer.astro:23`) add `+1` to *both* tracks. If 20 people sign up only for Track A, 10 only for Track B (`2.0:1` exclusive preference), and 20 followers sign up for *both*, raw totals become `40` vs `30` (`1.33×`), failing the `1.5×` threshold.
- **Evidence:** `promo-campaign-plan.md:11, 193–210`; arithmetic examples above; `handler.py:124`; `export_signups.py:35–41`.
- **Concrete fix:**
  1. Make pre-click randomized exposure the primary paid metric: **unique exclusive/first-choice signups per 1,000 impressions** and **per €100 of equalized arm spend**, tested with a **channel-stratified** estimator (e.g., Mantel–Haenszel across Meta, Google, and Reddit) rather than raw-pooling across Search and Social; treat `signups / page_views` as a diagnostic decomposition (`Impressions → Clicks → Page Views → Signups`).
  2. For organic traffic routed through `/`, define preference as **share of unique first-choice organic signups** (exact binomial test vs. 0.5), not `signups / track_page_views` (and reconcile `promo-campaign-plan.md:44, 94` vs. `201–202, 209`).
  3. Update `export_signups.py` (or add an analysis script) to group by `email`, report *Track A only*, *Track B only*, and *Both tracks* (using earliest `created_at` as first choice), and base the decision rule on exclusive/first-choice signups.

### 3. Page-view beacon drops `utm_medium` and `utm_term`, and paid ad arms share identical UTMs — merging paid and organic LinkedIn traffic and contaminating cross-arm attribution
- **Severity:** blocker
- **Where:** `/home/alexey/git/academy/src/scripts/signup.ts:69–74`, `82–94`; `/home/alexey/git/aws-infra-wt-academy/main/academy/lambda/handler.py:160–164`; `/home/alexey/git/aws-infra-wt-academy/main/academy/scripts/pageviews.py:29–32`; `courses/ai-engineering-school-germany/academy-landing/promo-campaign-plan.md:166`, `172–177`, `201–202`
- **What is wrong:**
  1. **Paid vs. organic LinkedIn collision in `/pv`:** `signup.ts:69–74` posts only `{ page, utm_source, utm_campaign, utm_content }` to `/pv`, and `handler.py:160` keys `PV#<day>` by `page|utm_source|utm_campaign|utm_content` (`utm_medium` and `utm_term` are discarded). In `promo-campaign-plan.md:166` and `177`, LinkedIn organic (`?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=track-test-2026-11`) and the €200 LinkedIn promoted post (`?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paid-social&utm_campaign=track-test-2026-11`) have identical `utm_source`, identical `utm_campaign`, and no `utm_content`. Consequently, **100% of paid LinkedIn page views and organic LinkedIn page views collapse into the exact same DynamoDB counter**, contaminating the `Organic, via home page` rows in the Result Table (`lines 201–202`) and Decision Rule #1/#2. Any organic Reddit/Google share with `utm_source=reddit` or `google` collides the same way, and dropping `utm_term` prevents per-keyword conversion analysis on Google Search.
  2. **Cross-arm contamination between paid ad arms:** In `promo-campaign-plan.md:172–176`, the Agentic and AI ad URLs on Meta, Google, and Reddit carry **identical UTM parameters** (e.g., both Meta arms use `?utm_source=meta&utm_medium=paid-social&utm_campaign=track-test-2026-11&utm_content=seats`). Because `Footer.astro:23` links to the other track (`Looking at the other track?`) and `Header.astro:12` links to `/`, and `signup.ts:82–94` appends the current UTMs to all internal links, a paid visitor from the **Agentic** ad who navigates to `/ai-engineering/` records a paid `meta` page view and signup on `/ai-engineering/` that is indistinguishable from traffic generated by the **AI** ad arm.
- **Evidence:** Live inline script on `https://academy.datatalks.club/` (`fetch(e+"/pv", ... {page:l(),utm_source:t.utm_source,utm_campaign:t.utm_campaign,utm_content:t.utm_content})`); `handler.py:160`; `promo-campaign-plan.md:166, 172–177`.
- **Concrete fix:**
  1. Add `utm_medium` to the `/pv` payload in `signup.ts`, store `page|utm_source|utm_medium|utm_campaign|utm_content` in `handler.py:160`, and export `utm_medium` in `pageviews.py`.
  2. Encode the originating ad arm in `utm_content` on every paid ad URL (e.g., `utm_content=agentic-seats` vs. `utm_content=ai-seats`, `utm_content=agentic-rsa` vs. `utm_content=ai-rsa`) so any cross-navigation between pages preserves intent-to-treat arm attribution.

### 6. Recommended €2,500 budget does NOT "show a 2 to 1 difference for certain": baseline CVR mismatch and zero click-loss assumption overstate power (~59–76% actual power for 2:1, ~33–52% for 1.5–1.6:1), while Decision Rule #4 hides an unapproved €1,250 overrun
- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** `courses/ai-engineering-school-germany/academy-landing/README.md:17`; `courses/ai-engineering-school-germany/academy-landing/promo-campaign-plan.md:34–50`, `58–78`, `86–88`, `211`
- **What is wrong:**
  1. **Mismatched baseline CVR between sample-size and budget tables:** To conclude that 450–550 visitors/page detects a 2-to-1 difference (`line 48`) and €2,500 *"shows a 2 to 1 difference for certain"* (`README.md:17`, `promo-campaign-plan.md:63, 87`), the plan relies on the `4% vs 8%` (`435` at 90%, `553` at 95% confidence, 80% power) and `5% vs 10%` (`343` / `435`) rows—which correspond to a **6.0%–7.5% mean CVR**. Yet the Budget table (`line 67`) assumes `3%` on Meta, `6.5%` on Google Search, and `2%` on Reddit, yielding a **blended mean paid CVR of only `3.62%–3.66%`** (`42–88` signups from `1,140–2,422` comparison clicks). At a `3.66%` mean CVR, a 2-to-1 split is **`2.44% vs 4.88%`**, which requires **`732` page views/page** at 90% confidence (`929`/page at 95%) for 80% power—not `435–552`. A 1.5-to-1 split (`2.44% vs 3.66%`) requires **`2,456` page views/page** at 90% confidence (`3,118` at 95%).
  2. **Zero click-to-pageview loss + LinkedIn clicks included in per-page totals:** Line 61 equates `1,180–2,470` raw ad clicks directly to `1,200–2,500` landing page views (`600–1,250` per page), including LinkedIn's `40–50` clicks (€200) that go to `/` and are marked *"Not for the comparison"* (`line 76`), and assuming **0% click-to-pageview drop-off** (paid social typically loses 20–30% of link clicks before page load/JS beacon). Removing LinkedIn (`570–1,211` clicks/page) and applying a 20–25% click-to-PV drop-off leaves **`428–969` page views/page**.
  3. **Actual detection probability is ~59–76% at 2:1 (never "for certain"):** Even 80% power misses a true effect 20% of the time. Simulating the plan's exact multi-channel setup (30,000 Monte Carlo trials at `3%` Meta, `6.5%` Search, `2%` Reddit, `10%` Organic), for a true **2.0×** ratio Decision Rule #1 triggers **58.6%** of the time at high CPC + 25% bounce (`66.8%` combining Rules 1 & 2) and **75.8%** at mid CPC + 20% bounce (`80.5%` combined). At **1.6×**, Rule 1 triggers **45.4%** (`51.6%` combined); at **1.5×**, only **25.8%–36.0%** (`33.4%–42.1%` combined).
  4. **Unbudgeted €1,250 extension in Rule #4:** Decision Rule #4 (`line 211`) mandates extending the campaign by 7 days at the same daily spend if either page has `<400` paid page views by day 14—implicitly authorizing up to **€3,750** (`+€1,250`) while `README.md:17` asks Alexey to approve **€2,500**.
- **Evidence:** Two-proportion formula check (`2.44% vs 4.88%` → `n = 732`/page at α=0.10, power=0.80; `n = 929`/page at α=0.05) and 30,000-trial simulation of `promo-campaign-plan.md:58–78, 208–211`.
- **Concrete fix:** Add `2% vs 4%` (`899`/page at 90%) and `2.5% vs 5%` (`714`/page at 90%) rows to the sample-size table, discount raw clicks by 20–25% for landing-page load loss, remove LinkedIn's €200 from the track-comparison click totals (or move that €200 into Meta/Search), replace *"for certain"* with honest ~60–75% power expectations at €2,500, and require explicit owner approval before triggering the Day-14 €1,250 extension.

### 8. Public Lambda API (`/signup` and `/pv`) lacks per-client rate limiting and strict origin enforcement, exposing the test to counter poisoning, address stuffing, and concurrency DoS
- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** `/home/alexey/git/aws-infra-wt-academy/main/academy/lambda/handler.py:24`, `72–73`, `154–178`; `/home/alexey/git/aws-infra-wt-academy/main/academy/academy.tf:87`, `100–110`; `/home/alexey/git/aws-infra-wt-academy/main/academy/variables.tf:19–23`
- **What is wrong:**
  1. **Bypassable origin check:** `handler.py:177` checks `if origin is not None and origin not in allowed:`, allowing any non-browser script that omits `Origin` (or spoofs `Origin: https://academy.datatalks.club`) to call `POST /signup` and `POST /pv` freely (`authorization_type = "NONE"`, `academy.tf:102`).
  2. **Availability DoS during paid ads (`reserved_concurrency = 5`):** Capping Lambda concurrency at `5` (`variables.tf:22`) bounds AWS spend, **not** availability—a trivial burst of ~60–100 concurrent requests saturates all 5 slots and causes real paid-ad visitors to get HTTP `429` (`That didn't go through`) while ad spend continues burning.
  3. **Unauthenticated `/pv` inflation & email security scanners:** Anyone can increment `PV#<day>` arbitrarily via `POST /pv` with a standard `Mozilla/5.0` User-Agent, and `BOT_RE` (`bot|crawl|spider|headless|lighthouse|preview`, `handler.py:24`) will not block corporate email security sandboxes (Mimecast, Proofpoint, Defender Safe Links) that open and execute JS on links when the DataTalks.Club newsletter is sent to thousands of corporate inboxes.
  4. **Honeypot-only `/signup` defense:** Direct JSON `POST /signup` calls omit `website` and can stuff fake or third-party emails into a small-sample test where 20–30 rows swing the outcome.
- **Evidence:** `handler.py:24, 72–73, 154–178`; `academy.tf:87, 100–110`; `variables.tf:19–23`.
- **Concrete fix:** Require `origin in allowed` (rejecting `origin is None`) on `POST /signup` and `POST /pv` (leaving `GET /health` open), place CloudFront + AWS WAF with a per-IP rate-based rule in front of the Function URL (keeping IPs out of application storage) and raise `reserved_concurrency` so a single noisy source cannot starve legitimate users, and count only Double-Opt-In-confirmed signups if anomalous submission spikes occur.
