# Strategic education partnership — DataTalks.Club Academy × Partner Academy

**Working discussion memo · 25 November 2026.** Status: proposal for negotiation, not an agreed deal or a legal opinion. Partner Academy is a placeholder for an established educational provider. The counterparty's name, personal contacts, website and private correspondence are deliberately omitted from this public research repository. The existing independent Academy launch remains an alternative; no exclusivity or commitment is assumed.

## Executive summary

Build a scalable AI Engineering education business, rather than simply sell course-development or teaching services. DataTalks.Club Academy brings curriculum, an established audience, a developing brand, initial demand and a founder willing to invest in a Berlin launch. Partner Academy brings an existing German education operation, experience with AZAV and established admissions, administration and student-support processes.

Start with one co-branded AI Engineering cohort in Berlin, with a properly approved operator arrangement. Retain DataTalks.Club Academy as a distinct brand and retain control of the underlying IP. Separate payment for active work from recurring participation in the economics of the program. Once the first cohort is validated, train and hire instructors, extend to other locations and corporate programs, and remove the founder from day-to-day operations within approximately one year. Preserve both a recurring-income route and an option to sell the economic interest later.

## 1. Context and negotiating position

The potential partner is an established education company with an existing German operation (including Munich) and international reach. Berlin appears to be an expansion opportunity rather than a new national market for the partner. The discussion follows an initial conversation and an expression of interest in collaborating. No commercial terms have been agreed.

An earlier review identified an advertised English-language AI Engineering bootcamp of around nine weeks covering RAG, agents, MCP, evaluation, cloud deployment and production systems; whether it has run, its next date, the status of its curriculum and its funding approval are open questions. Ask whether the partner wants to replace, improve or launch that offering, or create a new vertical.

The positioning is **a founder building DataTalks.Club Academy seeking a strategic partner**, not an independent instructor seeking a certificate or a job.

### DataTalks.Club assets

- An initial waitlist of approximately seven people (founder-reported; exact count, track preference, qualifications and funding readiness have not been confirmed). Early discoverability in AI search tools, including ChatGPT and Perplexity, is founder-reported and not a measure of conversion or verified demand.
- Existing AI Engineering Buildcamp teaching experience and positive learner feedback; a recent 5/5 review and an inbound inquiry about funded Weiterbildung are qualitative demand signals, not representative outcomes.
- Existing content, code, project-based teaching methodology, technical expertise and the AI Engineering Field Guide research (9,760 job postings, primarily outside Germany; see [research notes](bildungsgutschein/research.md)). These are inputs for curriculum development, not proof of Berlin demand.
- Local Berlin community, events, candidate relationships, founder availability to launch and teach the first cohort, and willingness to invest in local startup costs within an agreed budget.
- Ability to document delivery, recruit and train other instructors and provide academic leadership.

### Partner Academy assets (working assumptions to confirm)

- Established educational organization with a German market presence and experience offering funded courses.
- Existing processes for provider and new-course approval, quality management, admissions, funding applications, administration and billing.
- Potential student success, career services, recruiting, instructor management, infrastructure and B2B sales capabilities.
- Potential distribution through other German locations, international locations and live-online cohorts.

The complementary value is product/academic expertise, audience and local launch on one side, institutional execution and distribution on the other. An existing approval is not itself a transferable certificate or sufficient evidence that the new program is approved.

## 3. Three partnership structures

| Model | Description | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Co-branded program (preferred) | Jointly launch and operate a distinct AI Engineering program, share the economic result, keep IP and branding rights separately documented. | Both parties contribute substantial assets and expect to scale. |
| B — Operating partnership | DataTalks.Club Academy retains its brand, while Partner Academy is the approved educational operator and provides specific administrative, compliance and delivery functions for an agreed fee. | The partner wants lower investment and less commercial exposure. |
| C — Curriculum licensing | DataTalks.Club licenses a defined curriculum, prepares the partner's instructors and receives a development fee and recurring royalties. A and C can coexist: jointly develop the Berlin pilot; license the same program for other mutually agreed locations. B is a fallback where deeper co-investment is not attractive. Do not confuse an operating partnership with permission to rent or lend an AZAV certificate.

**Working branding:** DataTalks.Club Academy — in partnership with Partner Academy. Agree how both brands appear in the legal contracting entity, marketing, certificates, web pages and communications; avoid transferring the DataTalks.Club brand itself.

## 4. AZAV: gate before commitment

German funding requires distinguishing **Trägerzulassung** (approved provider), **Maßnahmezulassung** (approved specific program), and the approved **Standort** and delivery format. An existing German provider with voucher experience can potentially shorten the route and supply an established quality-management process; it does not automatically approve AI Engineering, the proposed duration, fee, Berlin site, online delivery or the founder's company.

The AZAV Beirat recommendations, version 17 June 2026, section 5.2, page 33, set a maximum of 10% for subcontracting an approved vocational training measure (FB 4) to a provider without the required approval, subject to statutory exceptions. The approved provider remains responsible for compliance. This is why a model in which Partner Academy supplies a certificate while an unapproved independent business delivers the entire measure is not a safe default.

Explore an arrangement in which the partner is the actual educational operator, the founder and future instructors are properly integrated into its delivery and quality-management system, and DataTalks.Club separately licenses IP and performs agreed commercial functions. A direct employment/teaching engagement is a candidate, not a blanket exemption. Obtain written confirmation from the partner's Fachkundige Stelle (FKS) and legal advisers before signing or advertising funded places. A separate joint company would not automatically inherit approval.

Questions for the operator/FKS:

- What legal entity holds approval, which Fachbereiche and sites/formats are covered, and when does it expire?
- Is an AI Engineering Maßnahme already approved? If not, what is the process, cost, documentation, lead time and approval owner for a new program or extension?
- How can Berlin and live-online delivery be added, and what evidence is required for space, technology, quality and attendance?
- How must the founder and future instructors be engaged? What is permitted regarding subcontracting, substitute teachers, participant contracts and academic leadership?
- What is the approved costing and B-DKS category? What happens if the proposed price exceeds the reference? Who bears audit/surveillance costs, cancellations and funding delays?
- Check VAT, cross-border contracts, employment/social security classification, GDPR, and FernUSG/ZFU where relevant. Do not assume eligibility of each student or a guaranteed voucher payment.

Primary sources: [§176 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__176.html), [§178 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__178.html), [§179 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__179.html), [§180 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__180.html), [AZAV §3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/azav/__3.html), [AZAV Beirat recommendations, 17 June 2026, p. 33](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/datei/dok_ba032840.pdf#page=33). See the existing [operations research](operations-and-azav.md).

## 5. Commercial model

Separate three income streams: (1) compensation for actual development, teaching, academic leadership and training of instructors; (2) participation in the continuing Berlin program economics; (3) curriculum royalties on additional locations where the program is used without the founder teaching. Corporate training can have its own pricing and allocation.

| Mechanism | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue share | Simple allocation of received tuition; useful when costs are clearly assigned and each party pays its own expenses. |
| Contribution-margin / profit share | Suitable if both parties invest and share direct costs; requires a transparent cost definition and accounting. |
| Per-student operating fee | Alternative where the partner supplies a defined package of operating services. |
| Curriculum royalty | Recurring compensation when other cohorts use licensed materials. |
| Development / teaching fee | Compensation for creation, delivery, instructor onboarding and any agreed ongoing updates. |

### Proposed opening positions (negotiation hypotheses, not market rates)

- **Berlin, preferred:** 50/50 of agreed positive contribution margin after explicitly budgeted direct costs, including market-rate instructor expense, development and agreed operating costs. Pay the founder separately for performed teaching/development, if applicable. Neither party should be expected to subsidize the other's share from its own unreimbursed startup spending.
- **Operating-only fallback:** 15–25% of collected net tuition to Partner Academy (75–85% to DataTalks.Club, from which it covers agreed direct costs), or a fixed per-student fee. This only makes sense if actual responsibilities, unit economics, risk and legal operator structure justify it; it is an alternative to the margin split, not an additional fee on top of it.
- **Other locations:** propose 10–15% of received net revenue as curriculum royalty where the partner's team delivers the program; development/adaptation and train-the-trainer fees agreed separately. Avoid charging twice for the same IP use in the Berlin pilot.
- **B2B:** separate agreement and revenue share depending on lead source, selling, development, delivery and support obligations.
- **Development and updates:** budget/fee for new work or approved expense reimbursement. Specify cadence and owner of future curriculum updates; recurring royalties must not require the founder to teach or provide unlimited unpaid updates.

Do not lead the exploratory meeting with percentages. First establish whether the partner is open to a strategic venture, what it will actually contribute, and its willingness to bear costs and risks. Agree the economics before making substantial commitments.

**Market context:** The Century Foundation's 2021 [study of US university–coding bootcamp contracts](https://tcf.org/content/report/flying-regulation-radar-university-partnerships-coding-bootcamps/) documents selected operator shares of 77–83.8% and varied tiered terms, with operators providing much of the curriculum and instruction. This is a historical US comparison, not a German AZAV benchmark, and the report raises concerns about oversight and student protection. It illustrates that arrangements differ with the division of work, not that a particular percentage is appropriate here.

### Illustrative unit economics (not actual partner costs)

Assume 12 paid or funded participants at €9,000 of net retained tuition, excluding any VAT due, refunds and uncollected funds:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Revenue | €108,000 |
| Instructor (founder or replacement) | €30,000 |
| Venue and local marketing | €12,000 |
| Partner operating services | €15,000 |
| Curriculum development | €8,000 |
| Other direct costs | €5,000 |
| Total direct expenses | €70,000 |
| Contribution margin | €38,000 |
| DataTalks.Club share at 50% | €19,000 |
| Partner Academy share at 50% | €19,000 |
| Founder gross compensation if teaching and development fees are both payable to the founder | €57,000 (30,000 + 8,000 + 19,000) |

The €57,000 includes work performed; it is not passive income or net profit. At eight seats, under the same simplified fixed-cost model, revenue is €72,000 and margin €2,000; at 15 seats revenue is €135,000 and margin €65,000. The approximate break-even is eight paid seats in this illustration, before additional risks or taxes. Actual costs include variable items; confirm with a real budget, cash-flow model and approved tuition. Margin must be calculated after agreed eligible costs, not after arbitrary central-office allocations.

Define which costs are reimbursed, which are fixed service fees, how contributions are recovered, who bears negative margin and when a cohort is cancelled. Track actual collected revenue, refunds, discounts, bad debts, delayed voucher receipts and VAT; determine payment timing, reconciliation, accounting access and audit rights. Require written approval for material budget changes, prohibit double counting of services and cap exposure to launch investment. Set a minimum viable enrolment threshold before committing to venue or staffing. The first cohort should be modelled using replacement instructor costs even if the founder elects to teach; otherwise profitability will disappear when the founder steps away.

## 6. Growth model and founder exit

The aim is a repeatable educational product with three routes: Berlin joint cohorts, licensing across the partner's existing network (including Germany and Switzerland), and corporate training using adapted modules. Additional territories and programs require separately agreed rights, capacity and economics. A general right to explore new markets is not exclusive ownership of every market.

| Milestone | Founder role | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nov 2026–spring 2027 | Founding partner / academic lead | Agreement, approval feasibility, program design, cohort economics, lead validation and launch plan |
| First cohort, target April 2027 | Founding instructor | Teach, gather feedback, document workflows, exercises, rubrics, teaching notes, incident handling and student support |
| Summer 2027 | Program director | Recruit/select a paid TA and qualified lead instructor, run teach-backs and handover, establish absence coverage |
| Autumn 2027 | Academic adviser | Next cohort delivered by the new team, founder limited to quality oversight and strategic decisions |
| Around Nov 2027 | IP owner / business partner | A repeatable operation, recurring contractual economic interest, or ability to negotiate a buyout |

The time line is an ambition, not a promise. Handover gates should include adequate demand, positive fully costed margins, qualified instructor and backup, quality/evaluation evidence, a named operations owner, documented admissions/billing/assessment processes, and a complete instructor guide. Do not promise that graduates can replace instructors without qualification checks. Measure successful delivery independent of the founder rather than a calendar date alone.

There are two different exits: stepping away from daily operations while retaining IP/royalties and economic rights, and selling those rights or the equity for an agreed buyout. Keep both options.

## 7. Legal and governance structure

Start with a strategic partnership agreement and a scoped pilot statement of work, with agreed milestones, contributions, approval conditions and economics. After a successful cohort consider an internal joint business unit; only explore a new joint venture/GmbH if sustained economics and expansion justify its legal and administrative cost. A hypothetical 50/50 JV is a later discussion, not the preferred immediate arrangement; define decision authority, reserved matters, deadlock resolution, capital requirements and approval implications.

Key points for a term sheet:

| Topic | Desired protection |
| --- | --- |
| Background IP | DataTalks.Club retains rights it actually owns; preserve third-party and open-source licenses. Partner receives a defined license, not ownership. |
| New IP | Define ownership and mutual license rights for newly created/commissioned content, derivative works, recordings, instructor guides and updates. Avoid ambiguous blanket joint ownership. |
| Brand | DataTalks.Club Academy, domain and audience remain independent; mutual co-branding permission limited to the approved program. |
| Scope | Initially AI Engineering, defined territories and modes; no broad AI education exclusivity. Agentic Engineering, Buildcamp, Zoomcamps and other products are excluded unless separately agreed. |
| License | Non-exclusive, defined duration, permitted cohorts and territories, restrictions on sublicensing and rights on termination; future instructors may use it under agreed conditions. |
| Economics | Defined net receipts, permitted cost allocation, reporting, payment dates, audit and reconciliation, expenses, loss exposure and no arbitrary overhead. |
| Lead and student data | Each party's marketing and student-contract responsibilities, lawful sharing, appropriate GDPR roles and notices; no transfer of the community contact database by default. |
| Academic quality | Instructor qualifications, curriculum approval, outcome review, learner support, feedback, replacement and quality standards. |
| Decisions | Named owners for academic, commercial, regulatory and operating work; budget approvals and escalation. |
| Termination and exit | Cure period, teach-out commitments, refund responsibility, ongoing royalty obligations on licensed use, return/cessation of materials and brand use, buyout mechanism and optional sale of the economic interest. |

Consider agreed valuation methodology or a negotiated multiple of sustainable earnings/royalties when there is sufficient data, rather than invent a valuation now. Ensure economics survive the founder's departure from teaching. Seek professional advice for German/Swiss cross-border contracts, liability, tax, employment and regulatory approval.

## 9. Decision gates and open questions

No specific percentage, equity split, budget, launch date, duration, eligibility, exclusivity or approval has been agreed. Confirm partner interest, exact provider/program/site approval scope, commercial decision maker, staffing and budget, enrollment and funding mechanics, and the FKS's written view on the proposed operating model. Confirm waitlist count and track-specific qualified prospects independently. The draft financial model and royalty ranges are examples only. Continue the independent Academy plan as a fallback until there is a credible signed partnership and approval path.

The desired outcome is an independently sustainable AI Engineering business with a protected DataTalks.Club brand and IP, distributed through a mature education operator, and long-term economic participation that does not depend on the founder teaching every cohort. The pitch should communicate scalable delivery rather than a desire to leave immediately; the transition and exit terms must nevertheless be explicit in the eventual contract.
