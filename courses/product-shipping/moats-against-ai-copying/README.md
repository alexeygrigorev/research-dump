# Moats against AI copying: research pack

**Question (t3273u, 2026-11-13):** anyone can now copy your AI product, or any product, using AI. How do you defend against that? The pack is for the January part of the Maven course *Product Shipping with AI* and for a promotional talk built on *Oversubscribed* lines.

**Status:** research draft. Nothing here has been published or added to Maven.

## Thesis

> **AI copies what it can see: code, features, UI, public content. It can't copy what it never sees: your users' outcome data, your audience, the trust people place in you, and the state your product holds for them.**

- The starting MicroConf talk (Craig Hewitt, MicroConf US 2026) ranks **first-party proprietary data** as the best moat, followed by speed, relationships and vertical focus.
- **The other moat talk at the same conference** is Anthony Eden's "AI Generated Apps vs SaaS: Which Moats Still Work in 2026". He argues that network effects, regulation, operational excellence and capex hold, while features, switching costs and integrations don't.
- Rob Walling's MicroConf Europe 2026 talk ("10 attacks, 8 moats") is the most complete framework.
- For a small founder the realistic stack is:
  1. an **audience you own**, because "AI writes the code. It does not write your distribution";
  2. **first-party outcome data** that compounds into a learning loop;
  3. **trust and vertical depth**;
  4. being the **system of record** for the customer's work.
- The honest caveat to teach: data is **not** a moat by default (a16z, NFX, Walling: "If you have a lot of data, doesn't matter"). It becomes one only when it is unseen, outcome-linked, compounding and sticky.
- Correction to the recollection: the talk does **not** name *audience* as a moat. That argument comes from other sources, listed in the source log.
