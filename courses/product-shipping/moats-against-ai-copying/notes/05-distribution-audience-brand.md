## Practical how-to-build steps for the distribution moat

Derived from the primary sources above. Each step names its source.

1. **Pick one channel and own it.** Choose one of: owned audience, SEO, paid, partners (Source 1 gives audience/paid/guerrilla; SaaS Club secondary says "own one distribution channel deeply").
2. **Build the audience before the product is final.** Levels: "almost nobody has distribution (like an audience)". Start building an audience during build, not after.
3. **Do manual outreach first.** PG: "You have to go out and get them." Recruit first users by hand and track weekly growth rate (Source 4).
4. **Launch with a specific initial audience.** Chen Growth Maze: decide "the initial target audience and launch strategy" before scaling (Source 6).
5. **Make distribution reusable across products.** SaaS Club/Tibo (secondary): distribution systems ("SEO playbook, ads pipeline, influencer network") that every new product can use.
6. **Treat good-enough product + distribution as the bar.** Chen (Source 6): "when great distribution can couple with 'good enough' product to win."
7. **Pair distribution with community or data.** Levels Oct 2026 lists network effects and proprietary data as separate moats (Sources 2, 3). Distribution alone is one moat among several.
8. **Test the "clone-tomorrow" question.** SaaS Club (secondary): if a clone launched tomorrow at half price, what still brings customers next month? If the answer is nothing, invest in distribution.

## Risks and counterpoints

- **Circularity:** Levels and Chen are both selling their own audience or reach. Distribution advice from distribution owners is biased.
- **Incumbent edge:** Chen (Source 5) notes incumbents with distribution may win the AI race ("Maybe incumbents that already control network effects and distribution will have an advantage"). A startup-friendly reading of "distribution wins" does not hold for everyone.
- **Survivorship:** Tibo's, Levels', and Marc Lou's results come from people who already have large audiences. The advice may not transfer to founders without one.
- **Rented reach:** Platform algorithms, app stores, and AI answer engines can remove distribution (secondary summaries of Lenny/Spiegel and Thompson raise this; unverified). Prefer owned audience (email, community) over rented reach.
- **Evidence gap:** None of the kept primary sources contains independent measured data on distribution vs product for AI-era startups. All are opinion or anecdote.
- **Dating:** Chen's essays are 2025; Levels's are 2026. The AI cost curve has changed since then.
