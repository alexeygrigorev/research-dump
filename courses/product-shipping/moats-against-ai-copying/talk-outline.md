# Promotional talk outline (draft — not published)

**Purpose:** a free talk, lightning lesson or webinar that pulls in a large audience for Product Shipping with AI, so that demand exceeds supply (*Oversubscribed*: lots of interest, then announce that the first cohort is capped at about 20 people, per Alexey's t3273u voice note). Related launch material is in [../oversubscribed-launch-plan.md](../oversubscribed-launch-plan.md).

## Title options

1. **"AI can clone your product in a weekend. Here's what it can't clone."**
2. "Moats for builders in the age of AI copycats"
3. "Your code is not your moat: shipping products that survive AI cloning"

## Format

30–40 min talk plus 10–15 min Q&A. Live-demo friendly. Works as a Maven Lightning Lesson, a DataTalks.Club webinar or a meetup talk.

## Outline

1. **Cold open (3 min). Live clone.** Point an agent at a small SaaS landing page and get a working clone. Ask the room: "So what's left?"
2. **The fear is real (3 min).** The "SaaSpocalypse", vibe-coded one-user apps, the "customer builds it" attack (Walling's 10 attacks). Name the attacks in one slide.
3. **What's no longer a moat (4 min).** Features, code, integrations alone, exportable data. One slide, with the MicroConf quotes: Eden, "The cost of creating code is going to drop, but the cost of creating good systems is going to increase."
4. **What AI can't see (8 min). The core of the talk.**
   - First-party data that never leaves your product, and why "more data" alone isn't a moat (a16z). Teach the four tests: unseen, outcome-linked, compounding, sticky.
   - The learning loop: "it's not fast-forwardable" (MicroConf Europe).
5. **What AI can't write: distribution (6 min).** Walling: "AI writes the code. It does not write your distribution." levels.io: everyone can build, distribution is hard. Alexey's own example: DataTalks.Club as a case study of an audience built over years.
6. **What AI can't be: trusted and accountable (4 min).** "People always have bought from people" (Hewitt); "someone to blame"; vertical depth.
7. **The clone test (4 min).** Give the audience the one-question test and a 3-line canvas to fill in on the spot.
8. **Bridge to the course (3 min).** "In Product Shipping with AI we don't just ship — each participant leaves with a product, real users and a moat canvas they've tested." Cohort facts go here: dates and price from Maven at the time of the talk, plus the seat cap.
9. **Q&A.** Collect the questions; they become course FAQ and lead-magnet material.

## Oversubscribed mechanics (suggested, not decided)

- Registration form asks one qualifying question: "What are you building, and what would a clone *not* be able to take?" This gives lead scoring and shows who is serious.
- The canvas is the lead magnet: download it with an email.
- After the talk: a waitlist / application for the capped cohort, then a "seats filled" update. Don't invent scarcity; only state the cap Alexey actually sets.

## Speaker notes on honesty

- Credit the MicroConf speakers by name. The first-party-data ranking is Hewitt's, and the "audience" moat comes from other sources, not his talk.
- Present data moats with the a16z/NFX caveat. Overclaiming "data is the moat" is the most common mistake in this topic.
